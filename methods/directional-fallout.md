# Directional ensemble with uncertain fan direction

The principal directional map traces the median thickness of fallout at each location. Measurements determine the local field in supported triangles. In poorly sampled directions, the model lets a fan point in different directions across possible deposits. The median can retreat from an empty sector while some draws still carry substantial ash there.

**Integrate the map for the principal bulk volume.** The integral covers the Earth. A second calculation integrates each complete deposit, then reports the median and conditional 95% interval of those volumes. Each draw keeps the same fan structure, orientation and concentration across the Earth. [Equations and numerical checks](../docs/methods/uncertain-azimuth.md)

Solid fragments require measured thicknesses bracketing the level and at least a 90% fitted crossing probability on the triangle. Dashes show where the median contour continues through a gap. Each case includes a near-source view, a whole-Earth map and the probability of reaching 10 cm.

Six base structures span three fan widths, each with and without an additional fan in the largest distal sampling gap. Other eruptions calibrate the weights, with the target excluded. Across six eruptions, uncertain fan direction slightly improved the pooled geographic score and worsened the score when whole quadrants were withheld. Each report retains the fixed-direction control and each validation design. [Six-eruption comparison](../comparisons/directional/README.md)

Qualitative archive evidence has its own sensitivity analysis. At Sihailongwan and Quanyang, the effect of finding shards without a continuous bed depends on how much local fallout would have left a recognizable layer. The primary map uses the audited quantitative observations and bounds.
