# Submit or recalculate an eruption through chat

Upload the thickness measurements and available source material with the eruption name. A spreadsheet, CSV or published table is enough to start. Include coordinates, thickness units, a vent location and any evidence for incomplete deposits, detection limits or redeposition. For multiple fallout fans, say which deposit the result should represent. Minoan combines its accepted Plinian and co-ignimbrite fallout in a single field.

Include the site list even where thickness cells are blank. A site name, core ID, source figure or field description can lead to a usable bound. The person or agent doing the calculation follows those leads and records the decisions. For a recalculation, begin with both the accepted input and the preceding ledger of omissions and proxy values. [How to review sites without measured thickness](methods/sites-without-measured-thickness.md)

Site-derived principal bounds belong in the accepted CSV. A separate archive inventory supplied through `--archives path/to/archives.json` can locate occurrence checks and define visibility sensitivities for the directional and spherical models. A core already fitted through the CSV may appear as a map marker, with its reading counted once. [Archive inventory and modeling options](methods/archive-evidence.md)

The [optional intake template](../templates/README.md) gives the observation schema. Leave unknown values explicit. Decisions about sources and deposit interpretation belong in the audit.

The default full run writes the principal footprint fit and geographic holdouts to `nested-isopachs/footprints/` and the original whole-area sensitivity to `nested-whole-area/`. Publish the integral of the median map, the integrals of the pointwise 25th and 75th thickness percentiles on that domain and geographic scores. [Footprint model and assumptions](methods/nested-footprints.md)

The directional model saves its principal fit and support results under `multiscale/`. The spherical model uses `extent/`. The nested family saves its matching results under `footprints/`. Each principal map and volume are in `deposit.png` and `deposit.json`. [Directional and spherical continuation](methods/finite-spread.md)


Preserve original lower-only records in the source CSV and generate point-at-minimum rows in the registered fitting input. Apply the same events in training, held-out evaluation and every model map. [Fitting and replotting reported limits](methods/reported-minimum-fitting.md)

## Auditing compilations and field records

**Reconcile the full catalogue.** A paper may publish a locality supplement, a shorter isopach table and a list of dated samples. Recover the legend and scope of each. For every locality row, preserve the original deposit code and link it to the principal observation, a duplicate's accepted ID, or a specific reason it cannot enter. Count source rows by class and decision, then count unique fitted sites separately. On re-audit, include earlier omissions and reassess inherited measurements and bounds against newly recovered evidence. [Mazama primary-classification crosswalk](../data/audits/Mazama/principal-ranges/buckland-primary.csv)

**Give the compiler's field assessment its stated weight.** Where the investigators explicitly classify a deposit as primary fallout and report a usable local thickness or bound for the target eruption, include it in the principal input by default. Record who assigned the class and whether the original log was independently checked. A missing original log is a verification gap. A conflicting horizon, a flow deposit, an unresolved location or a maximum taken elsewhere is a reason to revisit the value. For mixed deposits, look for a separately described airfall bed. For unclassified sites or sites outside the supplement, seek a field description that establishes emplacement. Record the evidence for eruption identity, primary deposition and preservation of original thickness separately.

**Search by archive and investigator as well as by eruption.** Trace citations both back to the observer and forward to later core studies or revised correlations. Search the layer's local name, site aliases and core or sample IDs in lake histories, geomorphic surveys, archaeological reports, theses and data repositories as appropriate. Check appendices, depth logs and original coordinates. Record which sources and rows a search added, which leads remain open and what is still missing. A regional marker age and a date measured at the locality have different evidential weight. Where a date anchors the identification, record the dated material, its position relative to the ash, and the reported age scale. [Mazama search record](../data/audits/Mazama/SEARCH_AND_GAPS.md)

**Investigate unusually thick distal beds in their field setting.** Distance, neighbouring sites and model residuals can flag a reading for review. Base the decision on the section. Inspect contacts, grading, interbeds, topography and variation across cores for evidence of direct fall, erosion, focusing or later transport. Repeated drives can confirm a thick package even where original fallout thickness remains uncertain. In Nevada, the investigators distinguished 8 cm of basal airfall at Overland Lake from 10 cm of overlying reworked ash. Record which bed supplies the fitted quantity and what supports any one-sided transfer. [Distal field comparisons](../data/audits/Mazama/distal-review/README.md)

**Identify what a pair of numbers measures.** Two contacts at depths of 177 and 200 cm give a 23 cm bed at Dog Lake. Two thickness limits on the same local bed give one interval. A range across named soundings needs the individual positions and readings and a reason to group them. A range for a region supplies no endpoint at an individual station without a site-specific link. Match apparently conflicting values by core, drive, depth datum, horizon and units before choosing a reading or an interval. Preserve the competing readings. Use an interval for them only when the evidence supports both as limits on the same thickness. For a multicore paper, list the drives, match them to stations and check that each accepted station appears in the input and on the observation map. Repeated drives at one station can corroborate the reading. Distinct stations in the same lake can each supply an observation. [Source readings and sections](../data/audits/Mazama/principal-ranges/source-notes.md) · [Interval and bound rules](methods/sites-without-measured-thickness.md#decide-and-encode-the-principal-input)

**Trace the observer and the measurement date within a table.** A single column can combine an eyewitness's accumulated fall, a later field measurement and a value copied from an isopach. Record which supplied each number. Check the eruption phases, rain, redistribution and erosion between fall and observation before interpreting an all-phase total as a ceiling on one phase. In marine surveys, reconcile a shipboard surface inspection with later radiography or a recovered bed at the same station. Retain the cruise, cast, inspection method and recovery of the sediment surface when two accounts disagree. [Pinatubo field re-audit](../data/audits/Pinatubo-1991/reaudit/README.md)

**Rebuild phase totals from the field logs when the mapping target changes.** Read the printed table and the full numbered-stop appendix together. Check whether a header is a combined package or a subunit, reconcile discrepancies at the same exposure, and record the facies evidence used to assign each component. For all-phase airfall, sum distinct primary fall beds once. A single preserved phase supplies a lower bound where other fallout could be missing. An author’s undifferentiated distal airfall observation can constrain the all-phase target without an assignment to a particular pulse. Keep older phase-specific inputs and estimates under their original names. When reinterpreting a historical unit could materially change the field, test the competing interpretation. [Ilopango field table and appendix](../data/audits/Ilopango-TBJ/field-logs/README.md)

**Identify the field exposure before combining layers.** A monograph may reuse a section letter, a chapter may misprint a position, or a compiler may use a regional coordinate. Compare the stop number, station map, stratigraphic sequence and topography before merging two accounts. If a later study reassigns beds to the target eruption, review every original section that contains those beds. Preserve the original unit names and record the emplacement evidence separately from the age decision. Record a measured thickness tied only to a slope or sector with its spatial support. [Millennium field sections](../data/audits/B-Tm/two-phase/README.md)

**Calibrate each panel that supplies a thickness.** An expanded log and an adjacent overview may use different vertical scales. Check shared contacts and independent field measurements before carrying a scale through the overview. Preserve disputed contact readings and any numerical bounds they support. At sites where shards migrated below a visible bed, record the preserved thickness and the migration depth separately. [Peat profile calibrations](../data/audits/B-Tm/two-phase/peat-profiles.csv)

## From upload to published case

1. **Audit the sources.** Preserve original files and hashes and record row identities and units in the case audit. Review each actual site lacking a measured thickness. Trace important locations to their original descriptions, calculate any defensible limits, and document the missing physical quantity or the demonstrated low geometric influence for an omission. Save the calculations and the links from source records to fitted rows. [Site review worksheet](../templates/SITE_EVIDENCE.md)
   For a redrawn survey, inspect the original colour legend. Link newly measured points, copied literature points and additions in the redraw to separate symbol IDs. Use independently located matches to check the spatial registration. Where a scaled section has an identified target bed but the total depth differs between accounts, retain the contact picks, scale bar and alternative calibrations. A finite union of physically plausible readings can supply an interpretive interval.
   Where a value or coordinate appears to survive only in a figure, document the table search for that field first. Publish each figure-derived reading with its numbered location, original close-up and fitting decision. [Map digitization audit](methods/map-digitization.md)
2. **Register the accepted input.** Save the reviewed CSV in `data/eruptions/` and register its path, audit and applicable spherical geometry in `data/datasets.json`. Distinguish directly measured values, reported bounds and interpreted bounds. Carry the same accepted observations into all five families.
   **Publish principal input figures whenever you register or revise the CSV.** Run `python -m benchmarks.principal_input_figures ERUPTION_SLUG` to rebuild `eruptions/<slug>/observations/` and link its README from the case home. Keep every accepted row on the overview and on a numbered, labelled detail plate whose extent is boxed on the overview. Inspect the plates at each scale for legible keys, values and bounds in dense clusters. Give each detail a latitude–longitude grid and a regional locator, including details entirely on land or at sea. Show the evidence ID and audited source for each row. Flag missing IDs and citations and keep complete references in the site index. Show the reported qualifier and both range endpoints alongside the actual fit event, including reported minima used as points. Verify the figures and input hash with `python -m benchmarks.principal_input_figures --verify ERUPTION_SLUG`. The maps can be published as soon as the input is registered. [Examples](../eruptions/README.md)
3. **Fit the models in a fresh scratch directory.** The GP ensemble and spline tune inside training folds and integrate a common hull. The directional ensemble infers uncertain fan headings, their variation with distance and structure weights entirely inside each training set. Check the proximal, intermediate and whole-deposit contours together. Retain the matched fixed-direction control, earlier ensemble calibration and single-structure sensitivities. The spherical mixture integrates its pointwise median-thickness field over Earth, with finite measured bearings and uncertain continuation. Publish its matched broad control, prior and hidden-fan sensitivities and all numerical warnings. [Spherical bearing protocol](methods/spherical-bearings.md) Bayesian nested isopachs give the third complete-Earth estimate, with per-draw sector and spatial volume budgets, fixed prior sensitivities and a map of the median surface. Run the paired directional gap-amount and spherical source-displacement checks on the same accepted sites. All three global families predict the same geographic holdouts. [Directional multiscale fit and historical controls](methods/directional-multiscale.md)
4. **Inspect the evidence.** Check mapped observation coverage, the source-region maximum, contour rejections, held-out predictions, sampling diagnostics and integration convergence. Preserve unscored folds, failed fits and prior sensitivity. Identify where a global volume relies on extrapolation even when the integral converges. [Output guide](OUTPUTS.md)
   For influential interpreted sites, compare local thickness, contour reach and volume with the site or linked group omitted and with a weaker response where appropriate. Inspect held-out readings at their own coordinates. Report the principal uncertain-fan-direction median map and its integrated whole-Earth volume first. Keep the fixed-direction and alternate archive responses in the sensitivity section. [Directional multiscale protocol](methods/directional-multiscale.md)
5. **Publish one case home.** Put the reviewed output in `eruptions/<slug>/results/`. On the case home, identify the accepted rows and source audit, include the three global median-thickness maps, the common regional map and the five-family table, and link once to each substantive report. Include any material limitation specific to the eruption. Link the case once from the eruption catalogue. Add a cross-eruption study only when the new calculation supports that comparison.

The complete run writes a plate and area table per global method, a regional map and `principal-volumes.csv`. Its README opens with the comparison. To reproduce that section for the case home, call `comparison_section(results_path, prefix="results/")` from `src.empirical_isopach.visualization.comparison`. Keep case-specific geology and source decisions beside it.

For a revision, record the prior commit, input and model identity and explain what changed. Preserve the scientific evidence needed to reproduce or challenge the result. If a revised input is published ahead of its model run, label both editions on the case home. Link the new principal input and its audit, and identify the exact input revision or hash and site count behind the displayed maps and volumes. [Publication and history policy](maintenance/README.md)

For a partial rerun, list the reviewed families in the current registry entry’s `published_methods` and place their packets under the case’s `results/<family>/`. The case home and family catalogues use this input and show the fits available on it. When a fixed benchmark still reads the preceding five-family edition, record its directory as `matched_results` on that previous input. Preserve its measurements, predictions and numerical checks together. [Worked editions](../archive/2026-11-24-case-editions/README.md)

Publish the spherical contour decisions and their field samples under `results/spherical-mixture/support/` within the case. The published-packet replay checks their hashes against that case’s accepted input and posterior.

When a case changes, rebuild the cross-eruption summaries from the saved fits and refresh the score figure for the methods published on that input. The summary-only commands retain the original fold scores and numerical warnings. [Comparison commands](../benchmarks/README.md#current-evaluations)

## Local equivalent

```sh
tephra-isopachs compare path/to/observations.csv --output results/new-eruption --name 'Eruption name'
```

The full directional report opens at `directional/README.md` inside the output directory. Its principal field, site predictions and conditional volume intervals are under `directional/multiscale/`. The preceding uncertain-azimuth and finite-spread fits are available as controls. The earlier fixed-direction calibration is in `directional/ensemble/` and the 13-policy reference in `directional/single-structure/`. The sibling `spherical-mixture/README.md` contains the independent global fit. The regional report is `README.md` and the local audit is `support/README.md`. [Installation and commands](QUICKSTART.md)

Publish the nested report, full and withheld-fit packets, geographic scores, prior sensitivities, deposition map, rank-based volume fractions and support screen under the nested case folder. Keep the directional and spherical sensitivity reports alongside their own families.
