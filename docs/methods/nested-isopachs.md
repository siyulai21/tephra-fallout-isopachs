# Nested thinning profile and whole-area sensitivity

The principal nested result fits intrinsic dispersal footprints jointly with the area–thickness profile. This page documents that profile, the original whole-area geography and its tests. [Principal footprint equations](nested-footprints.md) · [Current global estimates](../../comparisons/nested-isopachs/README.md) · [Original whole-area experiments](../../comparisons/nested-isopachs/whole-area-study.md)

The observations constrain a decreasing area–thickness profile and a geographic ordering in one likelihood. The profile gives the area above every thickness. The ordering allocates that area geographically. Changing an ordering while holding the profile fixed leaves the global volume exactly the same. Measurements can update both quantities together. Each draw represents the combined deposit across all fallout fans.

## Geographic ordering

Work on the WGS84 authalic equal-area disk with radius $D=2R$. If a location has score $s$, its rank is the equivalent radius $r=\sqrt{A(s)/\pi}$, where $A(s)$ is the physical area of all locations with scores at most $s$. A smaller score denotes thicker deposition. The entire disk has area $\pi D^2$. A contour consists of every point in the sublevel set, including disconnected components.

The fixed prior gives equal total probability to six shape families. They are circular, broad single fan, narrow single fan, turning fan, two fans and a deposit with a detached secondary maximum. Within each family the listed shapes are equiprobable, with 48 equally spaced rotations. The single-fan reach is a positive floor plus a power of the half cosine. Two-fan shapes use the greater directional reach. A turning fan rotates with radius by up to 90° and preserves area exactly. A secondary circular patch can lie 50, 250 or 1,250 km from the source. Some shapes allow its peak to exceed that at the volcano. These fixed shapes limit the patterns available to the model.

For each score, integrate the squared radial reach and add only the part of the secondary disk outside the primary fan. Integration over the angular arc subtended by the secondary disk resolves narrow, distant peaks. Clip both at the physical edge of the authalic chart. The rank lookup uses 768 positive scores and 512 midpoint bearings. Tests refine the angular integral and check rotation and volume against independent integrations. [Geographic equations and shape parameters](../../src/empirical_isopach/models/nested/geometry.py)

## Thickness and total volume

For equal-area radius $r$ and transition radius $L$, the inner deposit has thickness $T(r)=\exp(h-br/L)$ cm. A single observed bed contributes only its local thickness or bound. The curve above the break remains continuous and can flatten abruptly. The model estimates the core-to-distal balance directly. Direction, geographic shape, thickness scale and thinning break are all inferred from the target observations.

Let $x=r/L$ and $G(b)=\gamma(2,b)/b^2$, the dimensionless inner integral. Each draw takes a reference ratio $O$ of distal ash volume to core ash volume. For the exponential branch the distal thickness multiplier relative to $T(L)$ is $\exp[-(x-1)/s]$. For the long-tailed branch it is $[1+(x-1)/((p-1)s)]^{-p}$ with $p=2+\exp(z)$ and standard normal $z$. This law can thin very slowly over the measured range and has a finite integral in a reference area plane. Equal prior weights on the branches allow the data to update their relative probability.

The integral of the dimensionless distal profiles against $x\,dx$ from 1 to infinity is $s+Cs^2$, with $C=1$ for exponential and $C=(p-1)/(p-2)$ for the long-tailed branch. Solve $s+Cs^2=O\,G(b)\exp(b)$ for its positive root. This uses a reference area plane solely to set the thinning scale. The published volume integrates the resulting thickness over the **physical Earth**, up to $r=2R$. A tail reaching planetary distances consequently has a different realized distal fraction on Earth. The report calculates that fraction per draw. A global blanket cannot acquire arbitrarily large prior volume through a slope approaching a singular exponent. Thick and widespread fallout remains possible when the data support a large core or a large distal share. [Scale, inverse contours and finite-Earth integrals](../../src/empirical_isopach/models/nested/budget_profile.py)

## Independent information about the distal share

For a mapped two-segment exponential deposit, Table 1 of Paladio-Melosantos and colleagues supplies the proximal slope $k$, distal slope $k_1$, and first break in square-root area $B$. Set $b=kB$ and $c=k_1B$. Its continued distal-to-core ratio is $O=\exp(-b)(1/c+1/c^2)/G(b)$. This is a dimensionless inference from the published thinning slopes. The analogue calculation does not supply a target volume or a target dispersal distance. It assumes continuation of the first distal slope in those external profiles. [Original table, row selection and source digest](../../data/sources/nested-isopachs/README.md)

The donor estimates for the distal fraction are approximately 11% for Santa María, 73% for Quizapu, 71% for Novarupta and 54% for Hudson. The two Novarupta units contribute one log-averaged value. For the four independent volcanoes, let $m$ and $s_O$ be the sample mean and standard deviation of log odds. Under a normal donor population with unknown mean and scale and the reference prior proportional to inverse scale, the predictive log odds have a Student distribution with three degrees of freedom, centre $m$ and scale $s_O\sqrt{1+1/4}$. Its broad tails include deposits dominated by distal ash. The external sites are few and their mapped Plinian falls have limited transferability to co-ignimbrite ash. Reports remove each donor in turn, shift prior odds fourfold in both directions, and test a broader neutral transfer. The prior for the inner log drop uses the median of the grouped donor log drops and log standard deviation 0.9. The two analogue summaries are modeled independently.

Peak thickness in cm and transition radius in km have lognormal priors with natural-log centres log(100) and standard deviations 2.8 and 1.6, respectively. Log observation scatter has centre log(0.55) and standard deviation 0.65. These scales are common across eruptions. Other sensitivities broaden the peak, vary the prior odds of a long-tailed profile and upweight detached lobes. A fitted donor eruption would require leave-donor-out calibration for an independent performance claim.

## How the whole-area profile continues the last sampled isopach

For each posterior draw, take $m$ as the largest area-rank radius among the actual training locations, including accepted bounds. This quantity changes with the sampled fan geometry. Inside $m$ use the fitted curve without modification. Let $v$ be its integrated bulk and let $t$ be the thickness at $m$. The amount of thinning reached by the observations is $d=\log(T(0)/t)$.

Draw one of the four independent donor volcanoes with equal probability. For a donor curve $P(u)$ in units of its thinning break, find $u_*$ where $P(u_*)/P(0)=\exp(-d)$. Its ratio of remaining to interior volume is $H=\int_{u_*}^\infty uP(u)du / \int_0^{u_*}uP(u)du$. Geometrically average the two Novarupta units' dimensionless slopes first. Transfer $Hv$ as the reference outside volume, multiplying by a single lognormal factor with median one and geometric standard deviation two. The factor represents declared transfer uncertainty. Draw it once per complete deposit. Its quarter- and fourfold sensitivities show how strongly the total depends on transport assumptions.

Set the continued curve to thickness $t$ at $m$. Solve the same positive moment equation for its decay scale using this outside budget and area scale $2\pi m^2t$. At every actual training location the original and transferred profiles have exactly the same thickness and the same likelihood. Each whole-Earth integral joins the inner integral to the continued profile out to the antipode. A high fitted thickness can still produce a high volume through the mass inside the last sampled rank. The scheme admits large distal volumes when observations cover only the proximal fall. It treats a measured rank as a contour-shape assumption across unsampled bearings. A single faraway bed cannot validate that whole perimeter. [Joint continuation, finite integrals and complete-draw uncertainty](../../src/empirical_isopach/models/nested/continuation.py)

This construction is conditional on the documented survey design. Additional sampling may change where the model begins to borrow distal information. The calculation uses no user-drawn region or invented zero-thickness sites. A folded holdout rebuilds $m$, the transition thickness and the transferred amount entirely inside its training set. Archive checks distinguish bounds already used in the fit from independent occurrences. The development comparison preserves the original slope-prior fit and the unrestricted intrinsic mass-budget profile. The four donors were selected because the table supplies a mapped thinning break. Their suitability for diffuse and co-ignimbrite fallout is a structural uncertainty.

Beyond the largest equal-area rank radius $m$ of any accepted training site in each draw, an additional transport sensitivity multiplies thickness by $\exp[-(\max(r-m,0)/(fm))^2]$, with $f$ equal to 1, 2 or 4. The transition is smooth. The three choices are scale tests for distal removal during transport. No universal settling rate follows from them. Each gives exactly the original fitted likelihood and integrates to the antipode. The entire collection also runs in each geographic training fold to score genuinely withheld sites. The case tables show how much the resulting conditional volumes differ. [Distal continuation and numerical integration](../../src/empirical_isopach/models/nested/distal.py)

## Observation likelihood and numerical uncertainty

A measurement and a lower-only report each contribute a density at their stated log thickness. An upper limit contributes the probability below its threshold. The standalone nested runner also accepts positive ordered `Lower_cm` and `Upper_cm` with `Kind=interval` and uses one interval probability. The observation model mixes 95% ordinary Gaussian scatter and 5% with three times that scatter. A documented LogSD combines in quadrature with each component. Source rows marked pseudo are excluded by the shared reader. Separate archive probes use the recorded visibility assumptions and remain predictive checks.

Four independent sequential Monte Carlo populations fit each geographic training set. Within each population the two distal laws are fitted separately. Tempering advances at an effective sample size of 65%, followed by seven prior-corrected proposal sweeps. The tempering normalizers give the marginal evidence for each law. Independent populations start at 768 total particles each. The intrinsic-profile pilot doubles up to 6,144. The principal continuation checks both volume functionals and doubles the population whenever either check fails, up to 12,288 particles per independent population. The checks flag a ratio above 1.35 across population medians or above two across either 95% endpoint. These development thresholds measure numerical agreement. The reports retain all attempts, including nonconvergence at the ceiling. [Sampler](../../src/empirical_isopach/models/nested/inference.py)

An additional matched test withholds the farthest fifth of observations by source distance. It rebuilds each profile using the remaining buffered training sites. Results include poorly converged runs. The four buffered geographic splits match the regional, spherical and directional families. The shared contour screen uses 49 thickness levels, retained observation triangles and usable deletion fits. A missing supported band leaves the conditional global calculation available. Support fractions describe where sampling occurred. The thickness-slice table counts successive vertical layers at each location. The individual draw budgets add to the global volume. For example the first centimetre can contribute at most about 5,101 km³ worldwide. [Support screen](supported-isopachs.md) · [Whole-area catalogue and known-volume experiments](../../comparisons/nested-isopachs/whole-area-study.md)

Every case also tests the spherical model's finite reach in poorly sampled bearings while retaining the fitted thickness at each training site. The report gives the median and interval for ordinary and ash-rich hidden fans, paired geographic scores, compass-sector contributions, and numerical integration checks. This sensitivity changes angular extent and can change the ordering of contours. It is separate from the principal nested field. Four additional recovery tests use the actual Aso-4 and Ilopango survey coordinates, synthetic deposits with independently calculable total volumes, and a local archive-thickening scenario. [Whole-area case and recovery results](../../comparisons/nested-isopachs/whole-area-study.md)

The thickness is continuous at the last sampled rank. Its thinning slope can change there. Each case gives the probability that a particular source row sets that rank, including the same attribution among high-volume draws. An additional test removes the most frequent row, then removes its local cluster using the existing geographic buffer. It refits all nested parameters. These are sensitivity tests selected from the full fit. The buffered geographic folds remain the independent site-prediction evaluation. [Deletion protocol](../../benchmarks/nested_edge_deletion.py)
