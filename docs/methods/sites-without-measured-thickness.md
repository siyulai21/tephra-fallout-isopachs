# Sites without a measured fallout thickness

For every new eruption and every recalculation with revised evidence or methods, review real sites whose thickness is missing, qualitative or a proxy. **Include a defensible reported or interpreted bound in the principal dataset whenever the sources support one.** Omission from the quantitative fit needs a recorded reason. Either a best-effort investigation cannot bound the target deposit, or the evidence shows that the isopach geometry depends little on the site. Keep the occurrence, its provenance and that decision in the case audit. [Site review worksheet](../../templates/SITE_EVIDENCE.md)

## Find the sites and decide where to investigate first

1. Define the target, including the eruptive phases, primary airborne deposit, bulk-thickness convention and units. Reconcile the upload with the full published site lists, figure keys and earlier inclusion and exclusion ledgers. Look for blanks, zeros of unclear origin, trace or pod codes, dispersed shards, mixed units, incompletely recovered beds and author-assigned thickness equivalents. Treat conventions such as assigning every trace 1 mm as leads to investigate. Leave measured thickness blank until a field measurement is verified. Trace a legacy pseudo row to its origin if it might represent an actual observation. A point drawn to close a contour has no field observation behind it.
2. Locate each investigated core or section. Cross-check aliases, coordinates, datum, positional uncertainty, distance and bearing from the vent. Link repeated publications and overlapping samples to the same horizon. Preserve the compiler's row and the primary investigator's site ID. A maximum at a different section or a location assigned to a whole bog needs separate treatment. If plausible locations straddle a contour, compare them or mark that crossing unresolved.
3. Map the candidates against directly measured thicknesses and reported bounds. Prioritize an otherwise empty bearing, a fan margin or gap between lobes, a proximal thickness maximum, the last distal observation, and places where plausible contours disagree. A site can challenge an overly narrow fan as well as an overly broad one. Select the contour levels and sectors of interest before choosing a numerical bound. The model can identify a question to investigate. The site description and its physical controls supply the answer.
4. A shorter review is reasonable where geometry is already well constrained. Identify the nearby independent site IDs, their distances and measured thicknesses, the levels they bracket and the scale at which the candidate could move the contour. Where a bound is available, a paired omission comparison can demonstrate small changes in local reach or area relative to positional or numerical resolution. Similar whole-Earth totals alone say little about a fan margin. If influence is uncertain, investigate the site. Include an already defensible bound by default, even in a well-sampled sector.

## Read the original observation

Follow the compilation back to the primary site description. Inspect the text, supplement, core log, scaled photograph or concentration profile, as applicable. For an influential site with an incomplete description, follow the cited core or cruise report and look for companion sampling, density, recovery or geochemical data using the core ID and site aliases. Record the sources and versions inspected, page, table, figure or depth, and original file hashes where available. If a source is inaccessible, record the attempted route and the part still unknown. Leave the decision open to revision.

Establish what was actually recovered and how the horizon was assigned to the eruption. Distinguish a positive correlated occurrence, a documented search that found no recognizable bed, an unreached or missing horizon, and an unsurveyed location. Check recovery gaps, continuity, erosion, compaction, bioturbation, lateral transport, other eruptive units and the extraction size range. Record sampled depths, widths or area, dry or wet mass basis, blanks and positive controls where reported. For a digitized figure, preserve axis calibration, bin edges, scale, units, reading uncertainty and the extracted values. Record a positive identification even if the original loading remains unknown.

## Calculate what the source can bound

An ambiguous claimed thickness also belongs in this review. Separate a recovered primary component from a mixed total, a preserved bed from an assumed upland equivalent, and a reported range from a statistical error. A defensible endpoint can enter the principal input even when the total remains unresolved. Do not require an exact thickness before admitting a conditional bound; state the physical transfer and test it.

Start with the best measured quantity. Write its units and inequality, then show each step to the **original local bulk fallout thickness** $T$ in cm. Identify which numbers are reported, digitized, measured at a reference site or assumed. Each assumption needs a physical reason, a source or control that anchors its scale, and a statement of when the transfer could fail. Carry observed uncertainty, admissible ranges and rounding through the inequality. Label a statistical confidence limit with its coverage and a conditional physical limit with its assumptions. A measured control is preferable to an analogy. A sufficiently documented analogy can support a conditional principal bound. Round an upper limit upward and a lower limit downward.

| Source observation | Calculation to attempt |
|---|---|
| Incomplete primary bed, partial phase or mixed layer | A securely recovered primary portion can supply a minimum for the target, subject to the thickness convention and preservation. A mixed interval can bound the target's preserved share if containment is established. Account for compaction, redistribution and material outside the sampled interval before using it to bound original fallout. |
| Measured mass or shard counts through a profile | Integrate the event over its sampled window. For dry-mass concentrations $C_i$ (shards/g), dry host densities $\rho_{d,i}$ (g/cm³) and widths $\Delta z_i$ (cm), $N=\sum_i C_i\rho_{d,i}\Delta z_i$ gives recovered shards/cm². Use each sampled interval and size fraction once. Test bin widths, background, profile tails and figure-reading error. If host density is missing, $\sum_i C_i\Delta z_i$ is a concentration-depth index. It can use a compatible calibration expressed in those same units. |
| Dispersed clasts, patches, insoluble particles or selected size classes | Counts, clast dimensions, sampled area or a particle-mass profile can bound one recovered component. Document the shape assumption, coverage, size window and attribution. Converting to total fallout needs bounds on missing fractions, recovery and bulk density. A patch height, a slide percentage or the depth span of scattered shards alone measures something different from original bed thickness. |
| Inspected horizon with no recognizable bed | Look for a demonstrated recognition scale $d$ and a defensible minimum preserved response $f_{\min}$ in compatible units. Under reliable recognition above $d$, the implied upper scale is $d/f_{\min}$. For probabilistic detection, retain the calibrated chance of recognition or test explicit response scenarios. A sampling interval, a laboratory detection of individual shards and a visible-bed threshold describe different processes. [Archive recognition and predictive checks](archive-evidence.md) |

For a documented zero count, recover the amount actually searched and the extraction efficiency. For example, if aliquots sample a homogeneous dry sediment, recovery $e$ is known and a Poisson count is appropriate, zero identified grains in $m$ grams gives an upper confidence limit of $-\log(\alpha)/(em)$ grains/g at confidence $1-\alpha$. This follows from $P(N=0)=\exp(-emC)$ for concentration $C$ and negligible background. The result concerns the sampled size fraction. Retain the counting uncertainty and establish an archive-to-fallout transfer before using it to constrain an isopach.

Two conversions are particularly useful when the original bed has disappeared.

- **Mass budget.** If $M=f\,\rho_b T$ is the recovered *target* mass per area, $f$ relates the sampled target loading to original local loading and $\rho_b$ is original bulk fallout density. A defensible mass upper bound gives $T\le M_{\max}/(f_{\min}\rho_{b,\min})$ when both denominator minima are positive. The entire dry mass of a documented host window can cap $M$ if its included thickness and density are bounded. Conversely, securely attributable recovered mass permits $T\ge M_{\min}/(f_{\max}\rho_{b,\max})$ with defensible upper bounds. Incomplete capture reduces $f$. Focusing can make it exceed one. Account for local provenance and added material before interpreting a minimum.
- **Calibrated count response.** For a recovered index $J$ and an effective response $k$ per cm of original deposit, $J=kT$. An upper reading $J^+$ gives $T\le J^+/k_{\min}$ if the comparison supports a positive response floor. Match the eruption or material, sediment setting, grain sizes, extraction, units and sampling window. State what changes if the target archive recovers less than the reference. Where a response floor is unidentified, a proposed thickness $t>0$ requires $k\le J^+/t$ for a deposit at least that thick. Report that requirement as a test of the proposed contour. Calibrate the response from independent evidence.

## Decide and encode the principal input

- **Reported or interpreted quantitative bound.** Use it once in the common principal CSV for all five families. Keep the measured `Thickness` cell empty, `Pseudo` blank or `No`, and enter `Kind=upper_limit` or `Kind=lower_limit` with a positive `Limit_cm`. Link the CSV row to a stable evidence ID. Distinguish a source-reported inequality from a conditional interpretation in the ledger and case report. A wide, defensible bound is admissible. A borrowed value needs a documented transfer to this site. Leave unknown measurement error blank. [CSV specification](../../data/README.md)
- **Two defensible endpoints.** Keep both values, their units, provenance and dependency in the ledger. A true interval would contribute one probability $F(u)-F(\ell)$ under the local observation distribution. The current CSV reader supports one side per observation. Use the better-supported endpoint for the principal run, give the evidence-based reason before fitting, and test the other endpoint in a separate refit. Label this a one-sided approximation. A midpoint or two rows for the same horizon would change the observation. [Implemented observation kinds](../../src/empirical_isopach/core/observations.py)
- **Occurrence, component bound or scenario only.** When original local thickness cannot be bounded after review, preserve what the source *does* quantify and name the missing term, such as minimum capture, particle mass, source attribution or original bed continuity. Keep the location on the evidence map if known. A speculative transfer belongs in an explicitly conditional sensitivity or a held-out check. A confirmed arrival alone supplies no positive numerical lower endpoint. An unreached horizon supplies no absence reading.
- **Low geometric influence.** A decision to omit under this exception cites the local comparison above and identifies the contours or sectors to which it applies. Record any bound already calculable and the reason for omitting it. Proximity alone, the direction of a residual or agreement with a preferred volume cannot establish redundancy.

For a registered case, link the audit in `data/datasets.json` and record the accepted input hash under the chosen audit case. For interpreted upper limits, set `audit_case` to `principal` and list `evidence_id` and `interpreted_upper_cm` in the audit's `principal_limits`. Each ID matches a CSV row with `EvidenceID` and `Site`. A mapped archive uses the same ID and coordinates in the inventory with `in_principal_as` set to `interpreted_upper_limit`. Keep that ID out of scenario likelihoods. For a lower bound, use the ordinary `lower_limit` row and record its origin in the case ledger. [Registered B-Tm audit](../../data/audits/B-Tm/cryptotephra-principal.json) · [Corresponding map inventory](../../data/audits/B-Tm/cryptotephra-probes.json)

Freeze the case decisions, source and input hashes before comparing method performance. Mark shared cores, reference calibrations, alternative interpretations and possible duplicates. An archive listed for display can also be in the principal CSV. Apply its likelihood only once. Keep scenario-only sites out of the quantitative survey hull and measured interpolation triangles. [Archive inventory and refits](archive-evidence.md) · [Support rules](supported-isopachs.md)

## Verify the influence and publish the decision

Run the normal five-family comparison on the same principal observations. For influential interpreted bounds, compare with a fit omitting the site or linked group, and with a weaker physically explained response when transfer is material. Vary a shared calibration coherently. Report each scenario's conditional interval and identify which changes in response the source can support. Check at least the affected sites and bearings, using the same contour levels, map frame, vent and comparable numerical resolution. Report the local predicted thickness and interval, contour reach or area, and change in regional and whole-Earth volumes with their uncertainties. If an added site expands the observation hull, also compare regional volumes on the original common footprint. Treat a prediction outside a regional model's domain as unavailable.

Separate agreement after fitting from prediction with the site's buffered geographic region withheld. Use the local observation distribution for the probability of a bound and the latent field for isopachs. Keep common direct sites and folds comparable across evidence scenarios. A calibration estimated from the case belongs inside the training fold, or requires a separately labelled conditional check if it cannot be independently validated. Check for over-narrowing at independent occurrences and retain disagreements, poorly constrained sectors and numerical warnings. A fitted bound can change a contour without creating a measured bracket.

Before publication, reconcile the candidate list to one decision per physical site and horizon. Recompute each accepted endpoint from the cited inputs, match it to its CSV row, and check that all five principal reports used the same input hash. Link the ledger, source material, calculations and sensitivity results from the case home, including unresolved cases and justified low-influence omissions. For a recalculation, identify the prior input, audit and immutable revision, reopen earlier trace/proxy/exclusion decisions, and show what changed. A reproduction of a frozen historical calculation uses its recorded input.

## Worked decisions

- **An upper bound from a lake profile.** At B-Tm Lake Sihailongwan, the outward reading of the integrated glass profile is 14,212 shard·cm/g. Assuming a response of at least 250,000 shard·cm/g per cm gives an upper equivalent of 0.05685 cm, rounded outward to **0.06 cm**. The directional 1 cm crossing west of Paektu moved from 371 to 47 km after fitting all three interpreted cores. The two lake bounds share the Suigetsu reference. These response values apply to that case. [Source profiles and calculations](../../data/audits/B-Tm/README.md) · [Omission and weaker-response results](../../eruptions/b-tm/results/cryptotephra/README.md)
- **A lower bound from incomplete recovery.** The Minoan workbook gives 40 cm of Plinian fallout and 6 cm of co-ignimbrite fallout at POS513-25 with an incomplete total. The combined-deposit input retains **46 cm as a lower bound** at that site. [Minoan source audit](../../data/audits/Minoan/README.md)
- **An ambiguous total with a primary component.** At Mazama's Camp Creek profiles, the source distinguishes a 2 cm basal fine layer, interpreted as likely primary, from an 11–235 cm largely reworked unit. The reviewed input uses **2 cm as a conditional lower bound** per georeferenced section group, assuming that the primary component was not thickened by imported ash. It retains all profile identities, tests omission and a coherent weaker transfer, and does not assign the mixed total to fallout. [Mazama bound derivations](../../data/audits/Mazama/BOUND_REVIEW.md)
- **A quantitative observation that remains a map check.** At Ulleung Basin, 9,529 shards/g in a 2 cm bioturbated sample give a single-slice equivalent of 0.0762 cm under the comparison calibration. Sorting, lateral redistribution and the missing complete primary inventory leave total local fallout unresolved. The published case uses the occurrence to test the southern extent.
