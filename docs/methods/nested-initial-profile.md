# Initial nested-isopach profile

This page records the initial slope-prior experiment. [Archived case fits](../../archive/2026-11-21-nested-initial/README.md) · [Refined method](nested-isopachs.md)

The observations constrain a decreasing area–thickness profile and a geographic ordering in one likelihood. The profile gives the area above every thickness. The ordering allocates that area geographically. Changing an ordering while holding the profile fixed leaves the global volume exactly the same. Measurements can update both quantities together. Each draw represents the combined deposit across all fallout fans.

## Geographic ordering

Work on the WGS84 authalic equal-area disk with radius $D=2R$. If a location has score $s$, its rank is the equivalent radius $r=\sqrt{A(s)/\pi}$, where $A(s)$ is the physical area of all locations with scores at most $s$. A smaller score denotes thicker deposition. The entire disk has area $\pi D^2$. A contour consists of every point in the sublevel set, including disconnected components.

The fixed prior gives equal total probability to six shape families. They are circular, broad single fan, narrow single fan, turning fan, two fans and a deposit with a detached secondary maximum. Within each family the listed shapes are equiprobable, with 48 equally spaced rotations. The single-fan reach is a positive floor plus a power of the half cosine. Two-fan shapes use the greater directional reach. A turning fan rotates with radius by up to 90° and preserves area exactly. A secondary circular patch can lie 50, 250 or 1,250 km from the source. Some shapes allow its peak to exceed that at the volcano. These fixed shapes limit the patterns available to the model.

For each score, integrate the squared radial reach and add only the part of the secondary disk outside the primary fan. Integration over the angular arc subtended by the secondary disk resolves narrow, distant peaks. Clip both at the physical edge of the authalic chart. The rank lookup uses 768 positive scores and 512 midpoint bearings. Tests refine the angular integral and check rotation and volume against independent integrations. ## Thickness and total volume

With equivalent radius $r$, transition radius $L$, peak log thickness $h$, and positive proximal log drop $b$, the inner profile is $T=\exp(h-br/L)$ cm. At $r=L$ it joins either an exponential distal profile $T(L)\exp[-c(r/L-1)]$ or a power law $T(L)(r/L)^{-p}$. Each law receives half the prior mass. The exponent can fall below two. The Earth has finite area, and the integral extends to the antipode in every draw.

Whole-deposit bulk volume in km³ is $2\pi\,10^{-5}\int_0^D rT(r)\,dr$. A closed-form integral covers the core and both tails, including the removable singularity at $p=2$. Direct integration of the actual geographic fields with two independently scrambled whole-Earth quadrature rules checks this calculation. The displayed map is the pointwise posterior median. The scalar and its conditional 95% interval come from integrating complete deposits. [Profile, inverse contours and integrals](../../src/empirical_isopach/models/nested/profile.py)

The transfer information consists of **dimensionless shape parameters**. Table 1 of Paladio-Melosantos and colleagues lists the proximal slope $k$, first distal slope $k_1$, and break in square-root area $B$. The external analogue values are $kB$ for the core log drop and $k_1/k$ for the distal/proximal slope ratio. Average the two Novarupta units in log space first and take a median across four volcanoes. The model estimates the event's own peak and spatial scale. Pinatubo rows and published volume estimates supply no calibration values. These selected, historically well-mapped falls provide limited evidence about large prehistoric co-ignimbrite deposits. [Original source, exact rows and exclusions](../../data/sources/nested-isopachs/README.md)

The natural-log priors for peak cm and transition km are normal with centre log(100) and standard deviations 2.8 and 1.6. Log core drop and log distal/proximal ratio have standard deviations 0.9 and 1 around the external centres. The power-law exponent has a separate lognormal prior with median 3 and log standard deviation 1. Log observation scatter has median scale 0.55 and log standard deviation 0.65. These are declared modeling choices. Prior sensitivities weaken the transferred slopes, broaden the peak prior, alter the distal-law odds in both directions and increase the chance of a detached lobe. Reports retain importance-weight diagnostics. An externally calibrated model applied back to one of its own four donor eruptions would require a leave-donor-out evaluation for an independent performance claim.

Beyond the largest equal-area rank radius $m$ of any accepted training site in each draw, an additional transport sensitivity multiplies thickness by $\exp[-(\max(r-m,0)/(fm))^2]$, with $f$ equal to 1, 2 or 4. The transition is smooth. The three choices are scale tests for distal removal during transport. No universal settling rate follows from them. Each gives exactly the original fitted likelihood and integrates to the antipode. The entire collection also runs in each geographic training fold to score genuinely withheld sites. The case tables show how much the resulting conditional volumes differ. [Distal continuation and numerical integration](../../src/empirical_isopach/models/nested/distal.py)

## Observation likelihood and numerical uncertainty

A measurement and a lower-only report each contribute a density at their stated log thickness. An upper limit contributes the probability below its threshold. The standalone nested runner also accepts positive ordered `Lower_cm` and `Upper_cm` with `Kind=interval` and uses one interval probability. The observation model mixes 95% ordinary Gaussian scatter and 5% with three times that scatter. A documented LogSD combines in quadrature with each component. Source rows marked pseudo are excluded by the shared reader. Separate archive probes use the recorded visibility assumptions and remain predictive checks.

Four independent sequential Monte Carlo populations fit each geographic training set. Within each population the two distal laws are fitted separately. Tempering advances at an effective sample size of 65%, followed by seven prior-corrected proposal sweeps. The tempering normalizers give the marginal evidence for each law. Independent populations start at 768 total particles each and double up to 6,144. The checks flag a ratio above 1.35 across population medians or above two across either 95% endpoint. These development thresholds measure numerical agreement. The reports retain all attempts, including nonconvergence at the ceiling. [Sampler](../../src/empirical_isopach/models/nested/inference.py)

The four buffered geographic splits match the regional, spherical and directional families. The shared contour screen uses 49 thickness levels, retained observation triangles and usable deletion fits. A missing supported band leaves the conditional global calculation available. Support fractions describe where sampling occurred. The thickness-slice table counts successive vertical layers at each location. The individual draw budgets add to the global volume. For example the first centimetre can contribute at most about 5,101 km³ worldwide.
