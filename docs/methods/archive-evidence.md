# Using an inspected horizon to test an isopach

An archive can record ash from an eruption without preserving a measurable bed. A surveyed peat profile containing scattered pumice challenges a reconstruction of a continuous decimetre bed at that profile. A blank sector on a map supplies no comparable observation. Reconstruct original bulk airborne fallout, and record separately what was recovered in the archive.

## Audit the observation

| What the investigators established | Use in a reconstruction |
|---|---|
| A measured, correlated primary fall bed, or a documented limit on its thickness | Enter the value or one-sided bound once in the quantitative input. Preserve units, section identity and the reason for censoring. |
| The target horizon is securely located and inspected. Only microscopic ash or dispersed clasts were found, or a bed was explicitly sought and absent | Record the inspection in the archive inventory. Compare proposed isopachs with this site. An externally calibrated recognition threshold and defensible transfer to original fallout permit a one-sided likelihood. Without calibration, publish the threshold required for a conflict and sensitivity across stated transfer assumptions. |
| A source describes an occurrence but omits thickness or never says whether the horizon was inspected for a bed | Retain the positive occurrence or missing information. It supplies no negative visibility observation. |
| An age gap, erosion, incomplete coring, ambiguous correlation, reworking or an uninspected direction prevents interpretation | Retain the reason and spatial uncertainty. A blank area of the map remains an extrapolation. |
| An interval includes several units, non-fall deposits or substantial host sediment | Consider an upper bound on the *preserved target component* only when its containment is secure. Document any additional step from that component to original fallout. A maximum across several profiles requires the spatial extent and the identity of the measured section. |

Record the event and phase, coordinates and their precision, core/section ID, depth, inspection method, spacing, sampled size range, matrix, preservation, evidence for or against a continuous bed, positive controls, and a page/figure and source hash. A disputed horizon cannot enter a no-bed likelihood. Link repeated publications, adjacent slices and replicates to one stratigraphic event. Treat a positive shard identification as evidence of arrival under both weak and strong bed-recognition assumptions.

## What would the archive have detected?

Write $T$ for primary fallout thickness, $f$ for the net ratio of visible-equivalent preserved material to original thickness, and $d$ for the bed-recognition scale in the archive. Under monotone recognition, an intact no-bed reading can challenge $fT\ge d$. Focusing can increase $f$ and loss or dilution can decrease it. Core-slice width, shard diameter, recovery depth, peat thickness and a glass material density are different measurements from $d/f$.

1. Prefer an independently calibrated response for the same archive, material and inspection method. If a defensible numerical upper limit on primary fall is available, incorporate it once as a bound in all applicable families. If preservation or recognition is variable, propagate that uncertainty using measured controls.
2. Where only a comparison is available, record the positive control and the transfer assumption. A visible layer in another lake shows what that investigation could detect. Comparing it with a different lake requires compatible preservation, material and observation practice. Explicitly test weaker recognition and loss of the archive signal.
3. Score a hypothetical effective threshold $u=d/f$ against the **local predictive distribution**, including unresolved site variation. For an inspected absence of a continuous bed, the predicted probability is $P(T_{site}<u)$. A small value flags the joint field–archive interpretation at that location. Publish the curve or values at named scales, plus the effective threshold at the fifth predictive percentile. This last quantity is the transition for a 5% predictive conflict diagnostic. It is a property of the model. Fitting an archive and checking it at that same site measures agreement after conditioning. Holding a different archive out tests prediction.
4. If laboratory work establishes both a detection floor and a visibility ceiling for original fallout, use an interval likelihood, $F(u)-F(\ell)$, for a positive cryptotephra with no clear bed. Never give the same local deposit two independent measurement errors for these two conditions. If only the upper response is usable, preserve the positive occurrence on the map and report that very small predicted loadings remain unresolved.
5. An uncalibrated archive can reject a proposed **continuous preserved** bed or a particular extrapolation **under a stated transfer**. It cannot by itself determine a unique primary fallout thickness, a numerical bound at neighbouring sites, or an entire absent fan. Record which contour levels cross the site, the transfer needed, and whether the site was independent of the fit. Withhold a unique line where defensible continuations disagree. Show measured-bracketed fragments even when the rest of a level remains unresolved.

For two inspected horizons with uncertain preservation, write γ₁ and γ₂ for the chances that each bed-recognition signal is uninformative, conditional on the archive being present. Four refits use neither site, each one separately, and both jointly. The exact mixture gives each refit weight proportional to its prior subset probability times its predictive probability for its readings. Sweep both γ values from zero to one and show where the contour assessment changes. The B-Tm study reports this sensitivity without assigning a preferred loss probability.

For concentration profiles, the recovered inventory per unit area is $\sum_i C_i\rho_{d,i}\Delta z_i$, with $C_i$ in shards/g dry sediment, $\rho_{d,i}$ in g/cm³ and $\Delta z_i$ in cm. Converting this inventory to primary bulk thickness also requires a recovered size/shape distribution or representative masses, extraction and fine-fraction corrections, glass-to-total-ash mass fraction, bulk fallout density, and archive transfer. A count peak or a density from another core does not identify these quantities. A transferred shards-per-millimetre conversion can be a named sensitivity with its original calibration.

An independent count can also test excessive narrowing. If another study publishes a count-to-thickness conversion, evaluate the percentile of that illustrative equivalent in the model’s local predictive distribution. Report both the source authors’ equivalent and the result of applying the conversion to the audited count and its original units. Keep separate peaks or size classes distinct. Identify whether the archive supplied a visibility reading in the fit. A very high percentile at a held-out occurrence flags possible underprediction if the calibration transfers. It does not establish a physical lower bound. [B-Tm count comparison](../../eruptions/b-tm/results/directional/bearing/field-evidence.md)

## Continue the field into a broad unsampled direction

Build the interpolation footprint and candidate fan directions from the quantitative training subset. Archive probes and hypothetical missing fans do not create interpolation triangles. Retain the fully extrapolated six-structure result as a structural sensitivity. Also calculate a pointwise median using the three ranges of observed fan width and four cross-bearing range ratios $c=1,1/2,1/4,1/8$. These are model alternatives which include unrestricted radial continuation. They are common to all eruptions.

For each bearing let $\delta$ be its separation from the nearest quantitative training bearing. Evaluate the background at effective equal-area radius
$$r' = r\sqrt{1+(c^{-2}-1)\sin^2(\min(\delta,\pi/2))}.$$
The evaluation preserves the vent, values on quantitative bearings, and barycentric interpolation inside measured triangles. Quantified bounds retain their fitted likelihoods. Fan centres come from measured sites. The numerical area element remains the physical $r\,dr\,d\theta$. Whole-field volumes and sectors are reintegrated through the antipode. Retain coherent complete-field posterior intervals separately from the integral of the pointwise median.

Weights are regularized predictive stacking weights estimated on other eruptions, with each eruption and each available withholding design given equal total weight. Geographic and whole-quadrant withholding rebuild the geometry from training coordinates. A holdout design that samples positive deposits principally tests prediction *where ash was measured*. It cannot establish the prevalence of true non-deposition in an unsurveyed sector. Report any loss of held-out performance from narrowing. A suspected fan in an empty direction remains a separate structural scenario. The angular taper can leave a thin modeled ray beyond a station at the same bearing. Publish the actual measured-bracketed fragments and treat the extension of that ray as conditional.

In a conditional archive fit the likelihood of all readings, given one continuous deposit, updates both the parameters and the predictive mixture weights. Numerical bridge sampling relates each independently refitted posterior to its quantitative-only fit. Record the per-chain ratios, sampler diagnostics, coordinate identities and effective scales. Group, buffer and withhold archives with their regions before fitting geographic or quadrant deletions. Without identified probabilities for competing preservation assumptions, publish a median and a coherent interval for each scenario. The envelope of these scenarios or the area between their isopachs is a sensitivity range. [Bridge sampling](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/j6n4/j6n43/j6n43.htm) · [Importance-weight diagnostics](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v25/19-556.html)

Use the archive inventory linked in the case catalogue, or pass a JSON file with `--archives` to a full comparison or directional run. Its `sites` describe the actual inspections. Optional `scenarios` name the effective scales, assumptions, reference controls and intended sensitivities or held-out predictions. The unconditioned run always remains visible. Publish the case inventory, site probabilities, calibration eruptions, fold identities, numerical checks, sector volumes, supported segments and disagreement map together.
