# Recovering fallout observations when the table is missing

**Use figure digitization as a last resort for a specific missing field at a specific site.** A paper can publish thicknesses for some stations and show others only on a map. Recover each available value from the primary table, log or written field account first. When only the position is missing, take only the position from the figure. Apply this protocol before a figure-derived observation enters an eruption fit.

## Establish what the tables actually cover

1. Define the eruption, survey, sites, dates, layers, and quantity sought. Distinguish local bulk fallout thickness from mass loading, maximum clast size, component thickness and the thickness of an entire sedimentary section.
2. Follow the figure caption and data-availability statement back to the original survey. Check the paper's tables, appendices, supplements, corrections, linked data deposits, native spreadsheets or GIS station files, and relevant field, core or cruise reports. Search by eruption and survey aliases, author and station codes. Check whether another paper printed the original table. Transcribe a scanned table by row and column, including its headings and footnotes.
3. In the case audit, record the date, citation or query, identifier or link, places inspected, fields and sites found, gaps, and unresolved leads. Distinguish a documented lack of a table, a search that found none, and a known table still awaiting access. For a known table behind a broken link or access barrier, record the lead and keep that field provisional. If an archive documents the loss of a table, cite the record of loss. Name the sites and fields for which the figure is the surviving evidence. Reopen this decision if tabulated records surface.

| Evidence available for the target site | Audit decision |
|---|---|
| A primary table or field account supplies the value and location | Use that record. Link the map as a cross-check. |
| A value is tabulated but its station is located only on a map, or the reverse | Join the two fields by a documented station identity. Preserve a separate source for each. |
| Only an identifiable field symbol with a published reading, bound or calibrated section log survives the search | Digitize the missing field and verify it as described below. |
| Only isopachs, interpolated shading, model output, or an area–thickness curve survives | Preserve the geometry, levels, units and survey lineage as comparison evidence. A contour vertex, the space between contours and the area outside the outer line supply no site reading or bound. If this is all the evidence, report that a site-based fit is unavailable. |

## Freeze the source and inventory the whole figure

Keep the highest-fidelity original plate available, preferably the publisher’s native vector figure or scan, with its caption, legend, scale, ticks and surrounding methods. Record the exact edition, DOI or URL, page, figure and panel, original filename, dimensions and SHA-256. For a PDF render, retain the original PDF, page and crop box, rotation, resolution and the command or settings that reproduce the working image. All coordinates and crops must point back to the same version.

Decode the symbols for **this** survey. Record which marks mean field measurement, whole-section total, copied survey, sample for another variable, town, or contour label. Check the meaning of parentheses, superscripts, units and survey dates. Sweep each panel systematically and assign a stable, figure-specific ID to every candidate, including unreadable, duplicate and subsequently rejected marks. Retain those IDs through corrections. Reconcile the inventory with any published station counts and explain discrepancies. Where the source gives no total, describe coverage in terms of the inspected panels. For paired panels, store each panel's own symbol position and evidence that the stations correspond.

## Recover and check the position

- Prefer published station coordinates or a documented station register. Where the map supplies position, record the centre of the plotted symbol or the indicated end of its printed leader. Record the source pixel or vector page coordinates, origin, axis directions, and pixel-centre convention. Store crop and scale offsets. For example, the centre of the top-left pixel is (0.5, 0.5) under GDAL’s corner-based convention. [GDAL geotransform convention](https://gdal.org/en/stable/tutorials/geotransforms_tut.html)
- Register each distinct panel or inset in its printed coordinate system. Save the control pairs, their source, the datum or its uncertainty, the transformation and coefficients, and conversion to WGS84 longitude and latitude. Use distributed graticule intersections, ticks or independently located stations. Choose a transformation justified by the projection and scan. Check against identifiable controls held out from calibration wherever possible. Record their number, ground-distance errors including the largest, where errors concentrate, and sites beyond the control network. Identify residuals from fitted controls separately. With only the minimum controls, record that independent accuracy remains untested.
- For at least one site per registration, show the calculation from its original mark to the reported coordinates and overlay the result on the source. Record the local ground scale per pixel, marker footprint and repeated picks where available. Decimal places permit replay. Report what the map supports about location accuracy. Keep unlocatable or schematic sites in the evidence inventory with their positional qualifications.

## Read the observation and decide what it supports

Keep the literal label and units beside the interpreted value. Preserve inequality signs, decimal marks, ranges, footnotes and unclear characters. Verify the association among numeral, symbol and leader in its neighbourhood. OCR may suggest a candidate for inspection. The original pixels or vector marks supply the reading. Save the unaltered crop beside a reproducible enlargement. Resolve a faint digit from another verifiable rendering or source, or retain the ambiguity. Generated detail supplies no evidence. For a value read from a scaled section or graph, save the axis ticks, units and scale type, both picked boundaries as applicable, and the calculation. Carry the precision supported by the printed scale and line width into the reading or bounds. For mass or proxy measurements, document the physical conversion and its limits before assigning bulk thickness. [Site evidence and conversions](sites-without-measured-thickness.md)

| What the source establishes | Entry for the target fallout |
|---|---|
| A legible positive value for the target at an identifiable site | Convert the reported units to cm and use one `measurement`. Preserve source precision. |
| A reported `>` or `<`, or a defensible physical limit | Preserve the printed operator, including ≥ or ≤. For a minimum use `lower_limit`. For a maximum use `upper_limit`. Enter the positive endpoint in cm in `Limit_cm` and leave `Thickness` blank. Show the reasoning for an interpreted limit. |
| A measured component, mixed total, or paired sublayers | Sum only disjoint target layers at the same site. An identifiable, retained primary component can give a minimum. A complete measured total containing the target and additional material can give a maximum under documented preservation conditions. A **lower** limit on that mixed total alone supplies no maximum for the target. Carry missing components and inequalities through the arithmetic. |
| A published site-specific interval or a field symbol keyed to a thickness range | Preserve the literal range and its legend. When both positive endpoints bound the target fallout at that station, enter one `Kind=interval` row with `Lower_cm` and `Upper_cm`. Leave `Thickness` and `Limit_cm` blank. For an open-ended class or a partial or mixed unit, document which inequality transfers to the target. [Observation schema](../../data/README.md) |
| A blank, trace, printed zero, disputed digit, unclear symbol assignment, or unquantified occurrence | Record the legible evidence and investigate the site description. For a reported nondetection, look for a documented detection threshold and evidence that it bounds the target deposit. Enter a limit only if the evidence supports it across the unresolved interpretations. Otherwise retain the site and the competing readings in the ledger. Blank map areas supply no absence or zero thickness. |

Check aliases, repeat visits, erosion or reworking, reused survey symbols and sublayer or subsample totals against the primary sources. Preserve all source IDs and link copies to the one physical site and horizon. Use positional uncertainty and symbol footprint to flag possible repeats. Resolve them using station codes, survey history, symbol class and positional accuracy. A later map reproducing a field reading carries the same survey lineage. Explain conflicting values and the choice of source. Keep plausible alternatives available for sensitivity analysis.

## Make every decision verifiable by eye

Publish a visual check beside the case ledger. It should let a reader start with **any** candidate ID and reach the following evidence directly on GitHub.

- **Full figure.** Link the unmarked original and a numbered copy at full resolution. Point each number to its exact mark with an open ring or margin pointer. Preserve the printed label and nearby symbols. Cover excluded and unresolved IDs as well as accepted sites. Label both panels of a paired figure. On a crowded plate, give each ID a grid square and link it to its reading sheet.
- **Reading card.** Show an original-pixel enlargement with neighbouring symbols, the ID, panel and pixel position, literal reading, original units, symbol class, interpretation and decision. Use marginal pointers or a second marked view so the unmarked evidence remains legible. Include the relevant legend or link to it.
- **Review record.** Reread the original at the recorded position before comparing with the first transcript. Record both readings, the reader or review method, date, disputed characters or associations, and the evidence for the decision. Identify a repeat by the same reader as a self-check. Seek an independent reading for consequential ambiguities. Record a curator's proposed correction and check it against the source before accepting it. Publish the unresolved items together so the reader can inspect them while verified sites proceed.

At P7-028 a plausible numeral beside Botolan could not be assigned to an independent field site. All three can be located from the audit. ## Reconcile, replay and release

1. **Reconcile the ledger.** For each candidate retain its ID, source and panel, original position and literal reading, site identity, coordinate source and registration, units and conversion, observation kind and endpoints, review history, decision and reason, duplicate links, and accepted CSV ID if used. Reconcile candidate counts by figure and decision. Separately count unique fitted observations by kind. Each accepted row must trace back to its supporting records and each exclusion to a reason.
2. **Replay before fitting.** Regenerate coordinates, values, bounds, annotated maps and accepted rows from the preserved sources, picks and decisions. Check ID coverage, marker alignment, signs, decimal placement, units, coordinate order and joins across panels. Save the replay command and source, transcript and accepted-input hashes. Record uncertainty in reading and position separately from field measurement error. Leave unknown `LogSD` blank.
3. **Test influential ambiguities.** Freeze the principal decisions before inspecting model results. Where a map-derived site or group controls a fan edge, gap, proximal maximum or distal reach, compare omission and source-supported alternative readings, registrations or duplicate assignments. Vary a shared panel transformation coherently across its sites. Give all five families the same accepted sites and bounds for each scenario. Keep common geographic holdouts comparable and linked records together. Treat published contours built from the fitted survey as a consistency check. Report changes in local contour reach and volume separately from conditional model intervals, and state which sources of uncertainty each fit propagates. [Site sensitivity protocol](sites-without-measured-thickness.md#verify-the-influence-and-publish-the-decision)
4. **Publish and revise.** Link the search record, source plate, ledger, visual check and replay from the case audit. If a table becomes available or a visual review changes a reading, retain the previous revision and raw transcription. Match by ID, record old and new evidence and the reason, then refit the affected case when the accepted input changes. [Eruption intake and publication](../NEW_ERUPTION.md)
