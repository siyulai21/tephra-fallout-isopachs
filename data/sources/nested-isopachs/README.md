# External thinning profiles

Paladio-Melosantos and colleagues compiled proximal and distal slopes for several large fallout deposits in Table 1 of *Tephra falls of the 1991 eruptions of Mount Pinatubo*. These numbers describe published area–thickness fits. They supply external shape information and never enter a target eruption as thickness observations. [USGS chapter and Table 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pinatubo/paladio/index.html)

| Printed deposit | Proximal slope per km of square-root area | First distal slope | Square root of break area (km) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Santa María, 1902 | 0.021 | 0.0075 | 250 |
| Quizapu, 1932 | 0.098 | 0.0067 | 48 |
| Novarupta A, 1912 | 0.058 | 0.0140 | 47 |
| Novarupta B, 1912 | 0.053 | 0.016 | 46 |
| Hudson, 1991 | 0.180 | 0.0437 | 19 |

All non-Pinatubo rows with a proximal slope, a first distal slope and their break are included. Novarupta's units share a volcano and each receives half its weight. The Taupo row lacks a distal slope. Pinatubo is a test deposit. Hudson's third segment describes a later transition beyond the two-segment profile. Its omission limits what these analogues can establish about the finest fallout.

The refined profile also derives each external distal-to-core mass ratio and its remaining fraction at the relative thinning reached by the target. These dimensionless ratios depend on extending the published distal segment. They are transferred model assumptions with sensitivity checks.
