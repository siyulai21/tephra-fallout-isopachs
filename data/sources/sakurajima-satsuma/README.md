# Original Satsuma thickness sources

The Satsuma (Sz-S, P14) eruption occurred at Sakurajima about 12,800 calendar years ago. The accepted table uses centimetres of preserved bulk airborne fallout. Every location comes from published numbers. The audit links each field value, bound and omission to a site. [Observation ledger](../../audits/Sakurajima-Satsuma/selection.csv) · [Downloads, versions, byte lengths and SHA-256 hashes](source-files.json)

| Source | Evidence used |
|---|---|
| Nishihara, Geshi and Naruo (2022) | The authors measured pumice-fall thickness at 65 field sections. In Table S2, column T identifies P14 and columns D and E give location. Column C distinguishes sublocalities. The paper treats fallout and pyroclastic density currents separately. [Original workbook](nishihara-2022-tables.xlsx) · [Supplement, CC BY 4.0](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2022.988373.s004) · [Paper](https://doi.org/10.3389/feart.2022.988373) |
| Nishihara, Geshi and Naruo (2022), Geological Survey of Japan, AIST, Open-File Report 737 | The first worksheet lists 132 archaeological sites. W identifies P14 and D–E locate the site. The report’s native Word document identifies the source of each reading. It documents which sites used written descriptions or tables and which used a ruler or WebPlotDigitizer. The XLSX here is the original archive member. The ledger and its classifications are this project’s interpretation. [Original workbook](gsj-737.xlsx) · [Official report and archive](https://www.gsj.jp/publications/pub/openfile/openfile0737.html) · [GSJ use and attribution terms](https://www.gsj.jp/en/license/) |
| Moriwaki et al. (2011) | Sections 2.1, 2.2 and 3.1 give coordinates, thicknesses, facies and the glass correlation of the 8 mm marine bed MD98-2195-A and the 10 cm Tanegashima bed T-1-1. Yamamoto et al. independently print the marine coring coordinates. [Published readings and coordinate components](printed-sites.csv) · [Accepted manuscript](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/server/api/core/bitstreams/e039a717-fd75-4d37-9e87-5d057ab18304/content) · [Independent core coordinates](https://doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.863011) |
| Kagoshima Prefectural Archaeological Centre (2007) | At Nitaonaka A/B, volume 1, printed p. 11, layer VI, the excavators describe a roughly 70 cm primary P14 deposit with pumice at its base that persists across the excavation. This checks the western site 1B-24 against its upstream source. [Original excavation report](https://www.jomon-no-mori.jp/old/pdf_data/H46001-2-0110-1.pdf) |
| Konoo et al. (2024) | The field stratigraphy separates 17 units, including density-current unit P14B. Broadly dispersed Group 1 is followed by western and eastern fallout groups. The paper reassesses the location of the source. Its mapped observations lack a paired site-coordinate and cumulative fall-thickness table. [Stratigraphic study](https://doi.org/10.18940/kazan.69.3_115) |
| Sagawa et al. (2018) and IODP Expedition 346 | NK457 in Table 1 and Table S2 row 7 identifies Sz-S in U1429C. The 3–5 cm value is a sample interval. The 10 cm bed described on the shipboard sheet is in a different core section. [Tephra identification and supplement](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40645-018-0168-7) · [Shipboard core description, sheet 45](https://publications.iodp.org/proceedings/346/EXP_REPT/CORES/CORU1429.PDF) · [Site coordinates](https://publications.iodp.org/proceedings/346/109/109_.htm) |
| Sun et al. (2021) and Albert et al. (2024) | HGY1026 from Huguangyan in China has a published Sz-S correlation. The Lake Suigetsu cryptotephra SG14-1554 has a tentative correlation. The transcript retains the coordinates and sampling context for both. [Huguangyan](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020GL090582) · [Radiocarbon data](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.13522157) · [Glass chemistry](https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.13522142) · [Lake Suigetsu](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2023.108376) |

The source reference is the Japan Meteorological Agency’s Kitadake (Ontake) summit position, 31°35′33″ N, 130°39′24″ E. The exact prehistoric vent is unresolved. The site list preserves the precision of the published coordinates. Excavation coordinates locate an archaeological site and may encompass several trenches.
