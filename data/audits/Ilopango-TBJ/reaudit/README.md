# Ilopango Tierra Blanca Joven field audit

The principal input represents the upper widespread co-ignimbrite fallout, Unit G. The revision accepts **116 localities** with 74 measured or field-estimated thicknesses, 8 finite ranges, 7 lower limits and 27 upper limits. All thicknesses are local bulk centimetres. The sample includes original subaerial sections and qualified lake, marsh and marine equivalents. The 185-row ledger distinguishes observations, duplicate candidates, unresolved readings and occurrence-only records. Coordinates in the ledger retain the precision and limitations of the cited source. ## What changed in the field evidence

| Locality or source | Evidence used in this edition |
|---|---|
| Lake Yojoa, Honduras | Both Agua Azul and El Cacao contain chemically matched silicic ash. Mehringer et al. describe **2–3 mm**, giving **0.2–0.3 cm**. Their two horizon depths are 1.60 and 2.31 m. The one shared thickness description and lake position support one range. This replaces a lower bound that misrepresented the original description. |
| Petapilla, Honduras, and Amatitlán, Guatemala | The two contacts in Petapilla 2 differ by **0.35 cm**. A twig against the top gives 1540 ± 40 radiocarbon years BP. Lohse et al. describe **3 cm** of rapidly deposited AM16 ash at Amatitlán. Associated glass and core stratigraphy support both identifications. |
| Chalchuapa and the western highlands | Tazumal pits 6 and 7 preserve about **20 cm** of primary ash, with a distinct overlying secondary deposit. Pit 19 has about 17 cm. These support the workbook’s 20 cm at the named site. Casa Blanca trench 4N supplies **16–18 cm**. Dull’s four freshwater sites contribute three approximate thicknesses and the qualified **15–25 cm** four-core envelope at Laguneta El Trapiche. The nearby El Trapiche excavations at Finca San Antonio preserve a continuous ash with a **25 cm local maximum** below gravel. They are a separate archive. |
| Zapotitán valley and eastern wetlands | Conyers describes **30–40 cm** of TBJ above clay at Joya de Cerén, below the much later Loma Caldera deposits. The San Andrés primary-package maximum gives a conditional **52 cm ceiling**. Ash used for construction there reached metres in thickness. The six wetland sediment cycles at El Bable give a conditional **150 cm package ceiling**. Local erosion, redistribution or truncation could invalidate either transfer to original fallout. |
| Cerro El Carmen | Erquicia Cruz et al. identify G above F in the south wall of Operation 1. The wall scale gives roughly 117–145 cm across ten columns. Alternative total-section calibrations of 280 and 330 cm expand the upper endpoint to about 168 cm. Rounding outwards to tens gives **110–180 cm**. The published coordinate has impossible sexagesimal fields. The fitted position (13.73° N, 89.00° W) interprets those digits as decimal degrees and agrees with the described locality northeast of the caldera. | Kitamura field survey | Kitamura mapped 46 original fallout readings. Fourteen western-highland or eastern distal fine-ash sections enter as qualified measurements. Twenty mapped fall totals within reach of earlier phases enter as conditional upper bounds on G, including one queried reading corroborated by the later redraw. Nine possible aliases and three open-ended fall totals have no independent G constraint. The published flow map documents a different process. |
| Distal marine sites | Three distinct Dull map symbols add **>1, >5 and 10 cm**. The same figure and identified core records support **>5** at M66-223 and **>10 cm** at SO173-18. Primary glass correlation identifies C1 at additional casts, but mixing and recovery affect original thickness. The questioned Osa-margin reading remains an attribution query. |
| Workbook sections | The review preserves every original unit cell. Four capped sections with positive post-TBJ cover give conditional total-package ceilings on G. The other 35 zero entries lack evidence for retained original thickness or a calibrated search threshold. Two queried F/G contacts retain both 50 and 100 cm endpoints. Row P47 remains unresolved. Six previously excluded regional codes have individual published locations and quantities, including F1 at **30–50 cm**. Their positions are approximate. |

The stratum and the age evidence are recorded separately. The source ages include Smith et al.’s Greenland association at **431 ± 2 CE** and Dull et al.’s proposed **539/540 CE**. Most outcrops have an eruption correlation rather than an independent date. At Yojoa the authors report a combined **1810 ± 30 radiocarbon yr BP**. Kitamura reports project-level dates on flow charcoal and soil beneath fallout. Neither date supplies a direct age for each mapped dot. ## Reading the source evidence

**Workbook and excavation records.** Follow the input’s EvidenceID or the ledger’s input CSV row to the decision and original section. Hoja1 column H gives the total TBJ package, P the assigned G component and Q post-TBJ material. At a capped zero, a positive package total bounds original G only to the extent that the primary ash survived to burial. Subtracting the summed rounded layers would add precision that the contacts cannot support. The trench register separates intact horizons, secondary mud, fill and missing sections. ## Search coverage and unresolved deposits

The review followed source citations through eruptive stratigraphy and supplements, older Japanese and Spanish field maps, western Salvadoran excavations and wetlands, Maya-lowland lake studies, marine bed catalogues, drill-site recovery logs, cruise inventories and polar tephra records. Multiple deposits from a single locality were checked for common origin and later disturbance. Identical names can also refer to a lake and an adjacent excavation. Some land-map symbols lack station keys. Their thickness labels and alternative matches remain available for reassessment. The casts V15-26 and RC12-31 have positions and TBJ correlations but lack verified intact thicknesses here. DSDP 499 contains C1 glass in disturbed core-catcher material. The Costa Rican symbol is explicitly queried. None provides a site-specific calibrated detection threshold. [Unresolved locality decisions](localities.csv) · [Geolocated archive queries](archive-probes.json) · [Additional investigated records](other-field-records.csv)

Smith et al. recover 14–15 TBJ particles from a nominal 3.3 × 3.3 cm ice-core cross-section at TUNU2013. This yields **1.29–1.38 recovered particles/cm²** over a sample spanning about 2.5 years. The meltwater sample does not quantify individual particle volumes, recovery, primary phase or transfer to ground-level bulk thickness. The occurrence extends the known transport footprint. The model reports its prediction separately. Sulfur peaks, archaeological ash temper, unkeyed sites, unrecovered horizons and core catalogues lacking a confirmed TBJ bed carry no numerical zero. [Smith et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2003008117) · [Archive-site predictions](../../../../eruptions/ilopango-tbj/results/nested-isopachs/2026-11-24/archive-predictions.csv)

The western observations are much denser than the northeast and southwest. Marine sites constrain specific distal bearings, with uncertain recovery of podded ash. In the northeast the two point measurements are proximal and the Yojoa range is much thinner than the model predicts. The data leave large parts of the distal perimeter and the inter-sector extent dependent on the assumed thinning and tail.
