# Ilopango Tierra Blanca Joven, all-phase airfall

The principal table contains **168 localities** with 65 measured thicknesses, 14 two-sided ranges, 84 lower bounds and five upper bounds. The target is the local bulk thickness of all primary airborne TBJ ash, including fallout from co-ignimbrite plumes. The ledger identifies the source, position, eruption attribution, age evidence, preserved thickness and inclusion decision for 237 records. Duplicates and unresolved beds remain identifiable. The field studies correlate these deposits to the same eruption. Smith et al. place it at 431 ± 2 CE, while Dull et al. proposed 539/540 CE. The dates in early book titles record the chronology used at the time. ## Hart and Steen-McIntyre’s field stops

We recovered the editor-hosted scan of the 1983 book and checked **both pages of Table 2-1 and all 50 numbered stops in Appendix 2-A**. The table gives metres and distinguishes total TBJ, basal fine and coarse ash, T2, the surge, combined T1, T1 ashflow and T1 airfall. The last published column reports particle diameter in millimetres. Our transcription covers the elevation and thickness columns. The appendix adds contacts, estimated and preserved thicknesses, alternative readings, locations and sampling context. Figure 2-2 locates the field stops. Figure 2-9 on p. 22 codes upper ashfall with black circles and upper ashflow with white circles. It gives their facies at mapped exposures. The original investigators classified the laminated T2 as fallout. Their earlier preliminary report had interpreted several lower proximal units as flow-related. Modern mapping also resolves multiple density-current units. A historical T2 label has no universal one-to-one correspondence to a modern unit. The principal table follows the original local fall assignments. The independent nested refit removes every historical T2 contribution while preserving basal fall, upper fall and independently logged workbook minima. Stops 11, 22, 27 and 30 retain the published correlation or depositional uncertainty. The survey’s non-detections and degraded soils have no site-specific preservation and visibility calibration. ## The 1975 field season and 1976 laboratory appendix

The field report gives site and sampling tables, numbered sections and excavation accounts. Steen-McIntyre’s Appendix 1 is dated March 5, 1976. Its Figure 1 locates the samples, and its own Table 1 divides specimens into airfall, flow and reference groups. We traced all five listed airfall specimens. Santiago Texacuangos SS-12t has slightly more than 1 m of probable upper airfall. Its petrography and lack of scour support the ≥100 cm reading, with the flow alternative retained. Arce and Laguna Seca repeat named field localities. The 1971 Chalchuapa specimen lacks a distinct excavation position. CH-2 at Los Flores Mound 10 is an upper-airfall specimen without a reported retained thickness. The topographic-sheet position for Hacienda Los Flores is 14°01′50″ N, 89°02′50″ W. Fowler describes a thick ash cover. Its upper and lower contacts lack a measurable scale in the recovered mound plans, whose dimensions describe the structure. [Fowler site coordinates and excavation plans, pp. 172–177](https://biblioteca.utec.edu.sv/siab/virtual/tesis/55428.pdf) [1975 field report and Steen-McIntyre appendix](https://www.colorado.edu/anthropology/sites/default/files/attached-files/ilopango_volcano_and_the_maya_protoclassic.pdf) · [Record-by-record source decisions](preliminary-records.csv) · [Sample map and close-ups](preliminary-locations.jpg)

Río Grande lies about 2 km from Los Flores. Its 60 cm deposit covered cultivated ridges. The appendix groups the two CH-1 samples with the lower flow phase. Later summaries describe primary TBJ at the excavated field. The basin also supplies a 60 cm reading in the Dull/Pedrazzi compilation. S3 remains one qualified mapped reading; its exact relationship to the older excavation and the original airborne share remain uncertain. Comparable source problems affect the bulldozed San Mateo profile and the partly bioturbated Primavera cut. The supplementary decisions preserve the interpreted 30–40 cm at Longyear’s Los Llanitos Mound 10, whose eruption identity remains open, and 2 m at Cueva de los Fierros, where trapping and shielding complicate transfer from shelter fill to open-ground fallout. ## Reinterpreting the earlier Unit G table

The original Pedrazzi workbook records full sections. We sum separately measured A, B and G fall. A0, C, D and F are excluded current deposits. E has interbedded fall and current material. For three capped sections with terminal G and resolved lower beds (P27, P61, P77), the sum without E and the sum including all E bound the possible local airborne contribution. Partial or eroded sections generally give minima. A distant undifferentiated ash bed reported as the entire deposit can supply a bulk reading. A printed zero, an unexplored contact or a missing spreadsheet cell cannot establish zero fallout. [Workbook section transcription](../reaudit/workbook-sections.csv) · [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvolgeores.2019.03.006) · [Original workbook](../../../sources/ilopango-tbj/pedrazzi-supplement-1.xls)

Kitamura’s orange survey points describe total fallout. Three open-ended readings enter as lower limits. Three map-scale candidates within about 1 km of better localized Hart stops are grouped to avoid counting them twice. The registered coordinates and measurements for individual excavations, cores and survey symbols retain their original precision. At Cerro El Carmen, ten columns through the excavated wall bracket G above the current deposits and A+B beneath them. The 50 cm scale bar and the two published whole-section calibrations (280 and 330 cm) give extrema of 129.63 and 185.18 cm. The 120–190 cm input rounds outward and allows the thin basal A0 transition only at its upper end. Blue marks G, orange marks the lower fall contacts, and ochre marks the deepest permitted base.
