# Lake cores and field sections added after the Young review

Historical stage. The [current source decisions](principal-ranges/locality-decisions.csv) give the fitted reading and field qualification for each record in the 300-locality release.

The 230-row input at this stage included 27 additional site observations from this pass. Three previously listed Buckland localities were reopened. Pettit now supplies one conditional upper bound. Cooley and Rockslide retain both their published core-depth information and the compiler's bed estimates. The original data behind the 203-row checkpoint and its completed regional and nested trials remain identifiable in the [GitHub revision](https://github.com/siyulai21/tephra-fallout-isopachs/tree/734a8f2852331a585972a222699ed82e1c84064f).

| Locality or sections | Reading and use |

| Washington Thunder Lake, TL1D 54 | Visible Mazama glass at 335 cm depth. Two dated macrofossil levels at 320.5 and 440 cm enclose the surviving deposit. Their separation gives a deliberately broad conditional ceiling of 119.5 cm. |
| Ridley Lake, Washington, RY2B-4 | Visible Mazama glass at 206 cm in brown gyttja. Above is a separate Glacier Peak tephra at 166 cm. The underlying gray-clay unit ends at 277 cm. Their 111 cm separation caps the preserved bed. Both Washington reports match the Wasco glass standard at a similarity coefficient above 0.99. |
| Windy and Thunder lakes, British Columbia | The ash packages occupy 287–373 and 170–202 cm, respectively. The principal input uses 86 and 32 cm conditional on the dark-gyttja beds representing local fallout. The authors rely on stratigraphic correlation. The source refit treats both packages as upper bounds. Thunder Lake in Washington is a different lake. |
| Redmountain Lake, British Columbia | The split core contains no visible volcanic ash. The dated 409–520 cm interval spans the event, giving a weak 111 cm ceiling under continuous recovery and retention. The 0.5 cm layer seen at Windy is a different eruption in dark gyttja. It supplies no transferable visual detection limit in Redmountain's pale inorganic sediment. |
| Lower Frozen and Eagle lakes, British Columbia | The identified ash spans 155–163 cm at Frozen and 81–96 cm at Eagle. The paired core stratigraphy gives 8 and 15 cm observations. Lake-center coordinates and local dates accompany both. |
| Dismal, Rocky Ridge and Horseshoe lakes, Idaho | The preserved packages are 141, 161 and 211 cm. Herring directly identified potential focusing and slopewash at Dismal. The principal uses three ceilings, conditional on complete local capture. Horseshoe's parallel cores did not correlate beneath the ash. The shared weaker-transfer and omission fits expose that uncertainty. |
| Pettit Lake, Idaho | Buckland gives 6 cm. Shapley and Finney estimate approximately 50 kg/m². Shapley et al. distinguish primary airfall from much later, usually submillimetre remobilized silt. The input uses one conditional ceiling of 6 cm on the central composite. The 2007 holes have individual archive GPS records. The three cores do not count three times. |
| Redfish and Little Redfish lakes, Idaho | Two in-place Redfish horizons and one Little Redfish horizon fall within the original study's 2–8 cm airfall lithofacies envelope. Each enters as a conditional upper bound of 8 cm. RDF07-2A contains a displaced block and RDF07-3A an unconformity. Transport and erosion prevent using those two as measured fall or absence. |
| Mount Baker, Washington | The original NAD27 UTM sections distinguish primary fine-ash layer O from Mount Baker's much younger OP. RS–5, 16, 17, 18 and 21 supply minima of 2, 1, 2, 2 and 1 cm. RS–19 records approximately 3 cm. RS–15 caps the retained ash in soil at 20 cm. Other ponded, deformed or mixed units retain their source readings. Separate Osborn pits give a group maximum of 30 cm, Pocket Lake a 38 cm package ceiling and Easton Glacier only 1–3 cm deformed pods. RS–6 and RS–18 share a printed position despite distinct descriptions. Only the separate interfluve pit enters the fit there. |
| Three Sisters and Broken Top, Oregon | Site 43 is the northern Broken Top meadow. The 4 m sediment core contains 3 cm of glass-matched tephra at 330 cm. Whychus Creek sites 05 and 15 and soil pit 41 carry field-estimated lower endpoints of 4, 50 and 4 cm. The recoverable intervals from the figures are 4–9, 50–66 and 4–8 cm. At site 05 the two separated bands are never summed. Two sites contain likely reworked ash and four local sections or core groups have unassigned or younger units. |
| Cooley and Rockslide lakes, British Columbia | The original team's chronological appendix places Mazama at core depths greater than 377 and 221 cm. It does not specify the bases of intact primary deposits. The compilation's 252 cm Cooley and 30 cm Rockslide packages have core-fill or reworking problems. Both remain occurrence or component evidence. |

For the North Cascades ash mounts, a nominal 0.1 g across two complete 7 cm core footprints corresponds to 0.00130 g/cm² of recovered material. The reports describe several hundred milligrams prepared for analysis, and give neither the target mass fraction nor a lake-focusing correction for the preparation. This calculation characterizes the small recovered sample only. It would overstate local primary fallout if ash was focused into the hole. The much broader stratigraphic brackets have explicit preservation assumptions and use no invented recognition threshold. Site-specific outcomes and all numeric endpoints are in the [current locality ledger](revised-locality-ledger.csv) and [principal selections](model-selection.csv).

The original reports, supplement searches, private source fingerprints, local dates, map symbols and alternative endpoints have stable record IDs. Chapter and unit numbers for the Mount Baker sections are in the ledger. The numerical position table preserves the printed ticks and selected source pixels.
