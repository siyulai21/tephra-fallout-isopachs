## Original work queue and later resolutions

This queue describes the earlier 257-record stage. At the current revision 176 entries retain the compilation-only grade and 17 have Young’s compiled account without the underlying observer. Their actual recovery statuses are in the live ledger. Many still need retrieval, page-level checking, sample-level extraction and coordinate reconciliation. Personal-communication records require original logs or specimen metadata that are not contained in the published spreadsheet.

1. **Recover influential original tables and profiles.** The Young thesis has now been checked. Remaining leads include Lidstrom 1971/1972; Harward and Youngberg 1969; Rai 1971; Matz 1987; Kittleman 1973. These control proximal totals, corrected-upland values, Muir facies and several historical inconsistencies. Crandell and Mullineaux 1967 was subsequently recovered: its regional 3-inch maximum does not substantiate a Carbon River point measurement. Dates/titles of theses vary among citations; resolve bibliographic identity before treating versions as distinct evidence.
2. **Complete the 45 legacy-point primary checks.** The crosswalk preserves all 45 identities. The Fish Lake and Lost Trail primary measurements have since been recovered. Two of the four Long 2014 lake measurements have been checked in separate original studies. The remaining full logs include Breitenbush, Three Creek, Creston, Newman, Williams Lake Fen, Tepee, Squeah, Lulu and Lofty Lake. Primary passages for Lavington, Twobit, Portage, Mike, Lower Joffre and Green have now been read; exact core locations, chemical correlations and preservation still need qualification. Goldeye remains excluded because its2 cm compilation value is not substantiated by the recovered primary description. A correct article title, abstract or secondary quotation does not verify a particular thickness or core coordinate.
3. **Resolve individual samples and event identities.** Separate primary basal components from the total accumulation at Wildcat, Cooley and other mixed lake deposits; verify Jensen 2019 samples at Nordegg/Brule/Stampede and personal communications. Check older Glacier Peak assignments now attributed to Mazama, separate Llao Rock/Cleetwood/other precursor horizons, and retain the 2024 change in interpretation.
4. **Recover primary cryptotephra supplements.** The later review read Monteath’s Chapter 5 tables and text for Nuangola, Pound Cove and Southwest Pond, and the original 1999 GISP2 analytical paper. Remaining targets include complete raw profiles and the 1980 Camp Century source. Reassess MOR-T 12 using original Chambers 2004 analyses and an independent chronology. Do not treat the current tentative entries as confirmed records.
5. **Georeference new sections and possible duplicates.** The ledger reports exactly which records lack decimal coordinates; such records, generally because their primary sources supply native survey descriptions or maps instead. The 23 Montana entries retain their native PLSS locations. Camp Creek's seven profiles are now assigned to three documented section footprints; precise core positions still need recovery. Check overlaps with compiled Craig/Galata and nearby sites; retain within-site dependence rather than inflating sample count.
6. **Expand the remaining primary citation network.** The full 2020 bibliography is preserved, but its references are not all independently read. No numerical absence/detection inventory was established. Non-detection claims require sampled age coverage, sediment integrity, extraction protocol and a defensible detection quantity.
