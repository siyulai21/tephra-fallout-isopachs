# Mazama source audit — 22 November 2026

[Case and historical calculations](../../../eruptions/mazama/README.md) · [Data guide](../../README.md) · [Preserved sources](../../sources/mazama/README.md)

**The supplied 45 non-pseudo values are not 45 independently verified exact fallout measurements.** This audit reconciles the complete 293-entry Buckland et al. (2020) catalogue, maps the 52-row project input back to candidate source entries, and adds 59 records and leads from other literature. The resulting **352-record evidence ledger** distinguishes measured beds, components, ranges, traces, cryptotephra, tentative correlations, reworked deposits and unresolved records.

**Coverage limitation:** this is a complete reconciliation of the published catalogue plus a substantial, targeted primary-source audit—not a completed independent verification of every cited field source. Of the original 293 entries, **270 remain at compilation-only verification**; the other 23 have varying levels of primary checking, including original Buckland field descriptions, abstracts and partial checks. These are not 23 fully validated model observations. The [verification counts](audit-summary.json), [access gaps](access-gaps.csv) and [unfinished source checks](SEARCH_AND_GAPS.md) make that distinction explicit. The search is broad but cannot establish an exhaustive inventory of every Mazama occurrence.

No revised fitting input has been registered. The historical input, dataset registry and numerical results retain their original hashes. The revised dataset below is an **evidence dataset for source selection**, not an approved exact-thickness training CSV. `accepted_for_model=False` means “not promoted in this audit,” not “the locality is false.”

## What happened to the project table

The unchanged [supplied CSV](Mazama_supplied_52rows.csv) contains 52 data rows: 45 non-pseudo and seven explicitly invented constraints. Thickness is in centimetres. Its provenance did not include locality names or a source-row key.

All 45 non-pseudo values have plausible correspondences in [Buckland et al. (2020), Supplementary S1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00445-020-1362-1). These are **candidate lineage matches**, supported by location, thickness and selection pattern; the original digitisation/export history is unavailable. Twobit Creek is matched to ID 169 rather than ID 168, which shares its coordinates but lacks thickness. Lavington is matched to ID 205 rather than the slightly nearer ID 206, which also lacks thickness.

All 45 project coordinates differ from the candidate S1 coordinates: median displacement **1.216 km**, range **0.836–4.486 km**. This is not a demonstrated datum transformation. S1 coordinates themselves can be approximate, inherited, shared or inconsistent; their many decimal places do not establish survey precision. The ledger uses S1 as its declared source coordinate basis and retains conflicts. GISP2 is a separately documented inventory-based correction.

![Project coordinate discrepancies](coordinate-discrepancies.png)

The seven pseudo rows are retained only in the historical snapshot/crosswalk, never as new geological evidence. The 2020 article discusses eight inferred constraints; that is not the seven-row project set, and no missing pseudo point has been manufactured. The 2022 supplementary Table S4 has 30 listed primary localities and changes Jenny, Lower Joffre and Jesmond from 1 to 1.01 cm. Its main-text discussion of 36 primary localities is not a justification to merge different input lists or treat model inputs as newly measured data.

## Material source findings

| Record | Evidence and revised treatment |
|---|---|
| **Muir Creek /28; project row 52** | 850 cm derives from an 8.5 m exposed lapillistone in a radiocarbon locality description. That description does not establish primary airfall. Egan's thesis attributes a related Kittleman charcoal sample to tephra flow. **Quarantine the value from a reviewed fallout selection until facies and sample lineage are resolved**, rather than declaring it either a proven fall or proven flow. The original DMS position also differs from S1. [Valastro et al.1968, Tx-487](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033822200010985). |
| **Moss Lake /119; project row 22** | Egan 2016 thesis pp.203,215–216 reports **40 mm centrally and approximately 50 mm at the fringe**, with diffuse fringe contacts, and chemistry supporting climactic Mazama. S1 gives 5.5 cm and describes 5–6 cm. The ledger records 4 and~5 cm as core-specific values, not a hard uncertainty interval. The 151–158 cm sampling window is not a 7 cm ash bed. Older MLC-T480 is excluded from the climactic package. [Thesis](../../sources/mazama/egan2016-thesis.pdf). |
| **Juke Box /6** | Primary report p.11 gives a preserved layer varying **0–3 cm, averaging~2 cm**; Table 4 confirms glass identity. This conflicts with S1's 0.75 cm. Zero is a local pinch-out, not zero original deposition. Underlying anomalously old carbon dates are explicitly discussed as possible hard-water effects or an unconformity. [Oviatt et al.2018](../../sources/mazama/oviatt2018.pdf). |
| **Mount Bachelor /46** | 2020 total 64 cm comprises 56 cm pumice and 8 cm fine co-ignimbrite ash. 2024 field notes separate **44 cm UPU and 12 cm NLPU**, alongside a thin divider ash. The newer precursor interpretation changes the target-event question. Retain component evidence; do not silently label 64 cm climactic-only or subtract components inferred elsewhere. [2024 article](https://doi.org/10.30909/vol.07.02.853869), [Table S1](../../sources/mazama/buckland2024-s1.xlsx). |
| **Upper Kirk Road /B2024-UKR** | Additional field notes give **35–40 cm UPU**, overlain by 20 cm reworked/slope-derived pumice and soil. Exclude that overlying material from the measured UPU. This is a component observation, not necessarily total fallout; Fig.1 D also inherits some absolute thicknesses from Young 1990. Same 2024 sources. |
| **Jenny Lake /42** | Larsen 2016 Table 1 confirms S1's position as coreJEN13-1C. The geochemical sample at 93.5 cm is from **JEN13-2E at another position**. Both cores show tephra, but their coordinates, chemistry, sample depths and bed measurements must not be conflated. Compilation 1 cm remains pending core-specific thickness verification. [Larsen et al.2016](../../sources/mazama/larsen2016.pdf). |
| **Carbon River /107** | S1 says 5 cm in its numeric column but 3 inches (=7.62 cm) in its narrative. The recovered primary Table 1 p.8 gives a park-wide maximum of 3 inches in a regional column, not a Carbon River measurement. The reviewed point quantity is therefore empty; neither 5 nor 7.62 cm is accepted for this locality. [Primary report](../../sources/mazama/crandell1967.pdf). |
| **Day Creek /58** | S1's 59.5±12.5 implies 47–72 cm; its narrative instead says it averaged 72 and 40 cm, whose arithmetic mean is 56 cm. This is a source conflict, not a measurement-error distribution. |
| **Jesmond /229; Anthony /63; Simpson /53; Hurricane /68; Moscow /94** | Historical upland-equivalent adjustments, including factor-of-two changes and inferred removal of overthickening, are distinct from observed bed thickness. Do not average observed and corrected values or apply a universal compaction factor. |
| **Dick Springs /76; Meadowwood /77; Creston Bog /120** | Several historical measurements, corrected equivalents or core intervals coexist. Their source/core relationships need resolution; numerical summaries are not automatically exact observations. |
| **Wildcat Lake /103** | S1 describes a 1 cm primary component within a 146 cm total mixed/reworked accumulation. Preserve this as a candidate component pending the original log. The total cannot be used as primary fallout or assumed to be a rigorous upper bound on it. |
| **Mount Rainier /104** | Mullineaux 1974 pp.27–29 describes widespread 1–5 cm ash, locally 8–10 cm, and much thicker pond concentrations. These are regional descriptions, **not a locality-specific censoring bound** for McNeeley. [USGS report](../../sources/mazama/mullineaux1974.pdf). |

## Additional occurrences and their limits

**Securely reported cryptotephra does not supply bulk deposit thickness.** Lake Superior's second core near Isle Royale is retained separately from the compiled Keweenaw Bay core. Jensen 2021 adds Bloomingdale Bog, Sidney Bog core 2 and Thin-Ice Pond and reanalyses Nordan's Pond. Rabett 2019 identifies Balsam Creek BCK-284, while its older BCK-325 is not a climactic Mazama record. Monteath 2023 documents Placentia Bay 10G_195; Davies 2024 identifies Mazama in NGRIP. Coordinates, horizons, local or imported ages and geochemical qualifications are in the ledger. [Spano 2017](https://doi.org/10.1130/G39394.1), [Jensen 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2021.107242), [Rabett 2019](https://doi.org/10.5334/oq.54), [Monteath 2023](https://doi.org/10.5194/gchron-5-229-2023), [Davies 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quascirev.2024.108707).

**Redden Springs and North Redden are different grades of evidence.** Jones 2025 confirms Mazama glass at 99–100 cm in RSP15B by microprobe; NRS14 A at 65–67 cm has an XRF-index signal without shard/microprobe confirmation in the paper. Neither has a visible ash bed. A separate Great Salt Lake core has a visible 1 cm bed, but its position must be recovered from drilling metadata rather than copied from S1's different core. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1177/09596836251350232).

**Ireland is retained as a qualified reassignment.** Plunkett and Pilcher 2018 propose that MOR-T12 at An Loch Mor is Mazama rather than Hekla. Its interpolated age (~5300–5350 BCE) and reassigned chemistry need explicit reconciliation with the eruption chronology. It is not treated as an exact or censored thickness, or as proof that the intervening geographic area is supported. Nuangola, Pound Cove and Southwest Pond are retained as primary-excerpt leads; missing thesis/supporting data prevent a full analytical check. [Plunkett and Pilcher 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2018.02.006), [Monteath 2019 thesis](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/439312/), [Monteath 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JD042135).

**Patchy and sedimentary occurrences are preserved without converting them to fallout.** Lemke 1975 Table 4 contributes 23 specimen/locality records with native township–range–section descriptions, preserved thicknesses and sedimentary context. Decimal coordinates were not guessed; some may overlap broadly named compiled Craig/Galata occurrences. Several layers pinch out. HS-204 B and HS-206 occupy a shared section and must not be added as independent falls. The table's rounded foot-to-centimetre values and its internal ID/date inconsistencies remain explicit. [USGS Table 4, pp.H 20–H 22](../../sources/mazama/lemke1975.pdf).

Welcher 1993 describes seven Camp Creek profiles with a 2 cm basal fine bed interpreted as likely primary, below an 11–235 cm unit dominated by fluvial reworking. These are correlated profiles from one reach, with unresolved precise georeferencing, not seven new independent regional observations. Saltese Flats has a 71 cm geochemically matched bed, but its authors did not establish the amount of reworking or overthickening. Sunset Hill and Latah Creek are qualitative photographed occurrences. Billy Big Spring documents contrasting thick pond and microscopic shore preservation without numerical limits. Malpass clay is retained as weathered/redeposited material, not fallout thickness. [Welcher thesis](../../sources/mazama/camp-creek-thesis.pdf), [Saltese poster](../../sources/mazama/saltese2024.pdf), [Billy Big Spring abstract](../../sources/mazama/billy-big-spring2021.pdf), [BLM report](../../sources/mazama/baitis2015.pdf).

The Goldfinger NEHRP report adds eight records: Beaver, Sawyer, Tarboo and Bull Run lakes; Meyers Canyon; and three marine cores. It also supplies primary chemical context for compiled Leland and Spring Gulch. Its typical regional 2–7 cm description is not assigned to individual points. Tarboo core termination, a mixed Leland margin deposit and inconsistent marine sample depths remain explicit. The indexed primary report was readable but the PDF download returned 404, so these are labeled web-only checks. [G13AP00066, pp.20,49–51,62–66](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/cfusion/external_grants/reports/G13AP00066.pdf).

## Bounds, chronology and suitability for modeling

The [range/bound table](bounds-and-ranges.csv) separates **bounds on the preserved bed** from bounds on original fallout. Explicit “up to” or “less than” descriptions yield qualified preserved-bed bounds, for example Cypress Hills<10 cm, Mica Creek≤5.08/10.16 cm, Calgary≤10 cm and Brule approximately≤2 cm. These do not become atmospheric-deposition likelihoods merely because an inequality can be written down. Redistribution can add material, erosion can remove it, and weathering/compaction can alter thickness. No new unconditional original-fallout censoring limits were established here.

A trace, isolated patch, absence of visible ash, ash-rich soil, shard count, shard diameter, core sampling interval or chemical anomaly alone cannot determine a numerical bulk-thickness limit. Detection depends on sampling volume, preparation, grain-size recovery, concentration and preservation. No missing observation becomes zero. No shard-count-to-thickness conversion is attempted without the required mass, area, recovery and density information.

Chronologies retain their original meanings. Conventional radiocarbon years are not calendar years. Local age models are distinct from imported eruption ties, and old-carbon/reworking caveats remain attached. NGRIP's 7562±35 a b1.95k on GICC 05 is not silently averaged with GISP2's older 7627±150 estimate or later 7572±18 chronologies. All ice ages used here identify their 1950 datum. Unknown local ages remain unknown even where a Mazama correlation is reported.

Before a revised numerical comparison: settle the target package (historic combined fall versus climactic UPU/co-ignimbrite with precursors separate), resolve the influential source conflicts, georeference field/core records and document a justified common selection. Then register a separately named reviewed input and run GP, spline and the global companion on the same accepted observations and geographic folds, retaining every required support/prior diagnostic. Occurrence-only records cannot simply be appended as exact or censored measurements, nor can they extend a regional support hull without quantitative evidence. The historical volume reports have **not** been rerun or scientifically validated by this audit.

## Reproduction and checks

Run `python data/audits/Mazama/build_audit.py` from the repository. It uses the Python standard library, the preserved workbooks, [review decisions](review-decisions.json) and [additional records](additional-records.json). It rewrites only derived audit tables and checks the historical input snapshot. Source judgments are explicit data, not hidden parsing heuristics.

All 4,395 source cells were compared against an independent workbook extraction. Checks cover 293 unique source IDs, 52 project rows/45 candidate matches/seven pseudo exclusions, unique revised IDs, units, range/bound semantics and the absence of fabricated numerical cryptotephra limits. [Historical hashes](historical-input-result-hashes.json) cover the registered input, registry and 34 result files. The standard test suite passed 366 tests; the final navigation and hash checks are recorded below. See [validation record](validation.json) for final results.

The independent validation can be repeated with `python data/audits/Mazama/validate_audit.py` in an environment with `openpyxl`. It checks the original workbook with a second reader, verifies source and historical hashes, rebuilds the tables and checks navigation. The source-retrieval queue is a separate audit aid, not a model input.
