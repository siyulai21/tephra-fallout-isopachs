# Young’s field sections and distal Mazama ash

Young used two independent numbering systems. Table 1.1 lists 25 distant ash localities. Appendix 1 reports work at 138 numbered sections in Oregon, with separate entries for each eruption unit and several entries for most sections. An identical number in the two systems can refer to different places. The thesis is available through its [public catalogue record](https://ethos.bl.uk/OrderDetails.do?uin=uk.bl.ethos.315541). The [source manifest](../../sources/mazama/source-manifest.csv) identifies the 363-page copy examined by checksum.

## How much of the climactic deposit is represented

Young’s Upper Pumice Unit is a preserved climactic Plinian component. A measured component thickness supplies the same numerical minimum for the combined airborne deposit if the deposit is in place and erosion, compaction or omitted fine ash can only reduce the preserved bed. Imported pumice can invalidate that transfer. The section tables supply 103 geographically registered likelihoods after reconciling overlapping symbols and modern reoccupations. Precursor Llao Rock, Cleetwood and lower pumice remain in the field table. A “J” combines two pumice phases and a zero refers only to the specified component. Neither supplies an original all-phase thickness. [Primary-source phase reassessment](https://doi.org/10.30909/vol.07.02.853869) · [Every section decision](young1990-review.json)

The medial and proximal maps are schematic. Young’s equivalent Upper Kirk section supplies an independent registration check about 3 km from the nominal map position. The sensitivity moves all affected sections together. Distal positions have larger errors because the continental map has fewer controls and a much smaller scale. Candidate links in the crosswalk carry explicit uncertainty. A historical normalized value and a nearby named compilation entry share a likelihood only where the source and location support that decision. [Map registration and cross-checks](young1990-georeferencing.json) · [Alternative source selections](../../../benchmarks/mazama_sensitivity.py)

At Jesmond, Mathews’ Borehole 2 identifies a smear-prone, roughly 5 mm layer at 433 cm. The published borehole coordinates put it about 85 km from the compilation position. The fit uses Young’s 1 cm upland-equivalent ceiling, conditional on the density transfer and ash retention. The source sensitivity tests the original 5 mm estimate. At Lofty Lake the reported 1 mm bed anchors a lower-limit interpretation, with Young’s 2 mm equivalent ceiling tested separately. A trace symbol alone supplies neither a bulk density nor a recognition threshold. [Jesmond borehole and radiocarbon context, p. 227](https://emrlibrary.gov.yk.ca/gsc/papers/68-2b.pdf) · [Lofty Lake source coordinates, p. 113](https://emrlibrary.gov.yk.ca/emrlibrary/gsc/papers/72-1B.pdf)

## New observations outside Young’s tables

| Locality | Reported evidence and principal interpretation |
|---|---|
| Round Meadow, Oregon | Weatherford logged three vibracores, fen and terrace augers, and channel exposures. Core 3 preserves 200 cm of original graded pumice from 330 to 130 cm on the recovered-core scale. It is overlain by 10 cm of lag and 20 cm of floated pumice. The nine fitted holes carry separate minima and a one-core sensitivity. A second terrace description duplicates the same hole. Channel and outlet facies remain qualified. The reported 2.7–3.0 m regional blanket is spatial context. [Original field logs, Appendix E](https://web.pdx.edu/~i1kc/geolinq/thesis/texts/245-Weatherford-2015.pdf) · [Companion study](https://www.scirp.org/pdf/jwarp_2016102613224003.pdf) |
| Wilshire fen and section 5, Oregon | The lower reach at Wilshire retains 87 cm of lower pumice. The section 5 piezometer retains about 150 cm of eroded pumice. The first location uses the study’s graticule and fen-scale position. The second uses the surveyed land-section polygon. [Field study, pp. 1764–1770](https://doi.org/10.4236/jwarp.2014.619158) · [Section 5 coordinate derivation](walker-rim-section5.json) |
| Oregon Cascade lakes and fens | Indian Prairie yielded a 2 cm discrete layer, and the Gold Lake Bog core preserves 62 cm of pumiceous tephra provisionally identified as layer O. Gold Lake Bog is a distinct archive from the 2018 open-lake core. At North Twin Lake the possible basal positions of 224 and 241 cm, top at 143 cm and 5 cm fine tail give a conservative primary minimum of 76 cm. Lake focusing, older tephra and chronology offset remain material. [Sea and Whitlock, pp. 370–373](https://andrewsforest.oregonstate.edu/pubs/pdf/pub2181.pdf) · [Johnson, pp. 125–128 and Fig. 19](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6fc34314-524c-421c-892a-3ce2b721064a/content) |
| Stillwater Point Reservoir diversion canal, Nevada | Bell, Caskey and House reoccupied Morrison’s section and found a discrete 5 cm bed at 70 cm in an auger. Marsh or fluvial deposition and the historic lacustrine interpretation qualify its use as a direct point. The 6.7 ka organic date is from a different borehole. The geospatial map’s point-of-interest square identifies the location. [Map 168, Fig. 12B](https://pubs.nbmg.unr.edu/Geol_map_of_Lahontan_Mts_quad_p/m168.htm) · [Registration](stillwater-georeferencing.json) |
| Willapa Canyon, Washington margin | The 4 cm glass-rich horizon at 240 cm in BB326-36-PC is a possible airfall deposit on a canyon-head terrace. Boat navigation places the core at 46.48333° N, 124.70667° W, with approximate 0.5 nautical mile accuracy. Its 4 cm ceiling requires retention of the original fallout and negligible contraction. Export or compaction could violate it. Down-channel ash-rich turbidites remain separate. [Williams and Goles, p. 37](https://pubs.oregon.gov/dogami/B/B-062.pdf) · [USGS synthesis](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pp/pp1661f/) · [Station archive](https://pubs.usgs.gov/ds/2006/182/source_metadata/TechRep111/TechRep111.html) |

The cave contains a sheltered occurrence. The field accounts describe different environments and retain their individual measurements and limitations.
