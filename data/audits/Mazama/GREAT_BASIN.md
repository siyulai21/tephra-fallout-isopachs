## Source checks that affect interpretation

- At Oregon’s Fish Lake, eleven cores from five holes spanning less than 50 m² yielded one composite. The investigators explicitly describe 5 cm of tephra V. Major elements and mineralogy distinguish it from the older tephra VI. Their age model gives 6790 ±70 conventional radiocarbon BP. They discuss rapid settling and possible subaqueous flow. At Breitenbush Lake the investigator archive independently corroborates a tephra age control. Its 1 cm control width is insufficient to certify the compiled 17 cm bed. The Three Creek archive recovered here is a modern surface collection. Its locality footprint corroborates the lake but its sample cannot measure the Holocene bed. [Verosub et al., pp. 3610–3614](https://doi.org/10.1029/JB091iB03p03609) · [Foit and Mehringer, modern glass comparison](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/quaternary-research/article/abs/holocene-tephra-stratigraphy-in-four-lakes-in-southeastern-oregon-and-northwestern-nevada-usa/2CF58B4E6E4837F89276B5F0E3BA6062) · [Breitenbush investigator archive](https://api.neotomadb.org/v2.0/data/downloads/22406)
- Paisley Cave 2 preserves 35 cm of intact laminated ash over guano dated 6790 ±15 radiocarbon BP. Cave 5 preserves 30 cm near the mouth and 1–3 cm in the northern profiles. The original sedimentological study attributes accumulation and concentration in portions of the caves to water immediately after the eruption. Rapid thinning into the cave and unknown open-surface capture prevent an original bulk-fall bound. Cave 1 has a dated specimen in a Mazama context. LSP-1 is a separate northern Warner Valley shelter with small ash pockets in a mixed fan unit. These records preserve observations at the excavation scale. [Jenkins et al., chapter 28](https://pages.uoregon.edu/ftrock/pdfs/jenkins_geochronology.pdf) · [Jenkins et al., supplement, pp. 9–10 and Tables S13–S15](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1218443) · [Kennedy, pp. 110–125](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/547e5b5f-b02a-42ca-a1bf-371f4bae248d/content)
- Washoe Lake cores and trenches contain an eruption age tie in a sequence affected by desiccation, deflation and sheet flow. Four holes were drilled and the reports do not identify which hole supplied the ash reading. Reported sedimentation rates locate the age tie approximately, but cannot bound original fallout. The Wadsworth map assigns the Tsoyawata bed to Mazama. The USGS regional comparison separates Tsoyowata from the climactic ash and relates it to Llao Rock. The quarry and soil dimensions given in the map legend concern other materials. Diamond Pond is retained as a citation control because the modern multi-lake study specifically identifies the Mazama pair at Fish and Wildhorse. [Washoe drilling report](https://doi.org/10.2172/2571) · [Bell, Garside and House, actual Nixon terrace unit legend](https://data.nbmg.unr.edu/public/freedownloads/geospatial-pdf/M153_plate.pdf) · [Regional ash comparison in USGS Circular 1119](https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/1996/1119/report.pdf)

The group omission checks dependence on these eight quantitative records together. The second interpretation changes bed endpoints, lake capture, a map location and inclusion. Both retain the same survey-area comparison as the other source refits. The four dated Fish Lake Valley positions, the high lakes and Carp reflect different geomorphic settings and are retained as distinct source groups in the ledger.
