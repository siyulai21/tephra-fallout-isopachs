# Final citation pass and Lyman location review

[Audit](README.md) · [All locality decisions](LOCALITIES.md) · [Source manifest](../../sources/mazama/source-manifest.csv) · [Superseded 62-site trial](history/2026-11-23-before-final-citation-pass/README.md)

The final pass reopens earlier exclusions and follows the newly retrieved literature into other sites in the same studies. The principal selection has 67 observations: 43 point values, 23 conditional lower bounds and one conditional upper bound. This is a conditional source selection, not certification that every historical field log has been independently recovered. Each observation has a stable identity, source locator, position basis and preservation assumption in the ledger.

For each lower limit, the physical statement is `original local fallout ≥ recovered primary component`, conditional on correct correlation and no imported ash thickening that component. Compaction or incomplete recovery can reduce preserved thickness under that interpretation; neither justifies assuming zero focusing. The grouped half-transfer scenario relaxes the minima by a factor of two as an explicit stress assumption, not a measured correction. The upper estimate at Skull Creek requires the authors' original-fall interpretation; it is widened to10 cm in the weaker-bound scenario and also omitted individually. The five final additions are omitted together in a separate refit.

Dog Lake's older928–927 cm tephra, described as likely Llao Rock, is retained as a separate excluded event. Dog Lake Oregon is distinct from the compiled Dog Lake in Kootenay National Park. Skull Creek's younger0.5–2.0 mm unidentified layer is likewise an event control, not six Mazama observations from its six exposures. If the authors' two Mazama falls include a precursor, a ceiling on their combined nonnegative thickness still bounds the climactic share, conditional on the estimate itself.

Skull Creek's native description spans the southern half of section17 and northern half of section20, T36S R32E. The [georeferencing record](skull-creek-georeferencing.json) retains the enclosing BLM polygons and a derived centre. Full sections deliberately bound the described area; no trench precision or probabilistic location error is invented. The [mapped upper-bound inventory](interpreted-archives.json) uses the same identity and position as the fitted CSV; it supplies no second likelihood.

The Lyman thesis is the work titled *A Holocene glaciolacustrine record of the Lyman Glacier and implications for glacier fluctuations in the North Cascades, Washington*. Its chemistry, primary/slopewash separation and core dependence had been recorded before the location follow-up. It was not rejected as nonprimary. The EPA lake catalogue now supplies a defensible representative lake position. SNOTEL coordinates, moraine boulders, the glacier latitude used in climate modeling and same-name Okanogan lakes are not substituted for a coring site. Original PDF retrieval was retried through the university's advertised link, but failed; indexed primary methods, tables and appendices were readable. The referenced LacCore workbooks were not recovered.

The Montana facies review also remains explicit. Lemke1975 H27–H28 interprets the76 cm Galata bed as channel concentration by wind and water. At RWL-1, H24–H26 separates12 cm pure basal ash from18 cm impure ash, but the bed wedges out in a pond/slough sequence. Purity alone cannot bound transport of pure ash. A stream-bank exposure alone does not prove reworking, so other unresolved Montana records are not described as proven secondary falls. No original-fall bound is invented from their preserved totals.

Mehringer1987 was retrieved while following Skull Creek citations; it does not reproduce the needed Skull Creek scan. Its Blue Lake, Pine Forest Range appendix independently identifies the basal recovered ash as redeposited (pp.53–54, Table2), despite a later discussion of more than40 cm ash and a hoped-for deeper primary horizon (p.64). This is additional evidence for B2020-012, not a40 cm primary-fall minimum. The younger mixed-glass horizon at3.606 m remains separate from the basal ash accumulation.
