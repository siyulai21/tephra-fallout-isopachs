# What the thick distal records can establish

Buckland’s largest distal numbers include measured beds, transported material and compiled values with an unknown relation to original fallout. Distance was used to find records worth reopening. The decision for each record follows its stratigraphy, correlation and recovery. In particular, finding Mazama glass in a thick unit identifies its source. It does not determine how much of that unit fell directly at the measured position. [Original Buckland study and supplements](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00445-020-1362-1) · [Every locality and its interpretation](locality-facies-crosswalk.csv)

The review screened every original S1 numeric entry of **at least 30 cm at least 300 km from the vent**. All 29 appear in the linked register, alongside 16 further records flagged by their reported interval endpoints, measured values or fitted points/minima. These thresholds organize the review only. Records below them and thin layers within thick host sediments also appear in the full ledger. A figure depicting a concentration peak, a sampling interval or a map contour never supplies a measured bed by itself. ### Thin beds, unmeasured horizons and isolated patches

Brown’s Begbie Lake core gives a **1 cm** ash bed at 405–406 cm. Horrelt’s later paper reuses that record and adds three distinct cores from Frog, Swanson and Worley lakes. All four contain optically observed glass. Their individual event ties rely on stratigraphy, radiocarbon and regional dispersal. The latter three yield only **≤225, ≤268 and ≤190 cm** from physically sampled sections. These numbers are deliberately broad ceilings. Treating the age model’s zero-width event or a graphic line as measured thickness would be erroneous. An alternative widens the three envelopes using the physical amount of core outside the sampled early and late blocks. [Brown et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/0959683619854523) · [Horrelt thesis, pp. 30–34](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/bitstreams/92486c1d-6999-4795-ba5a-b66a99850a0d/download) · [Horrelt et al., Tables 1–2 and methods](https://doi.org/10.5194/cp-22-561-2026)

Gowan locates Mazama in the intact gyttja of Matheson, Prior, Pike and Maltby lakes at 137, 158, 201 and 158 cm depth. Scaled stratigraphy, including explicitly discarded core tops, gives enclosing-unit ceilings of **313, 127, 329 and 244 cm**. Thin horizontal horizon symbols in Fig. 2.4 have similar widths across different depth scales. At Florence Lake the corer failed twice to penetrate the thick ash. The latter observation has no complete local bed boundary. [Gowan, chapter 2, Fig. 2.4 and Table 2.1](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/bitstream/handle/1828/263/thesis-final.pdf) · [Florence Lake field report](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2013/rncan-nrcan/M44-2002-A16-eng.pdf)

Original survey field comments give **5 cm** in Mica Creek terrace peat and **10 cm** at Mt. Kobau Lake. The Mica date below the ash is inverted, and Kobau’s correlation depends on peat directly overlying the bed. Both enter as conditional upper limits and both disappear in the uncertain-identity sensitivity. The same date list identifies tentative shards in eolian sand at Ponoka, a higher ash-bearing alluvial terrace near Trout Creek, and separate Mazama and St. Helens Y horizons in the 1981 Goldeye lake core. The Goldeye lake and adjacent fen share rounded coordinates. At southwest Edmonton, a patchy ash has a broad **approximately 100 cm** enclosing section and a coarse published project location. The feature depth alone supplies no thickness.
