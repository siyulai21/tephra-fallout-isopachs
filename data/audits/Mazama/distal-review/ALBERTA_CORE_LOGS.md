# What the original Alberta cores preserve

At Johnson Lake, 1,044 km from Crater Lake, Buckland’s 100 cm entry corresponds to a much thicker accumulation than several nearby primary beds. Beierle’s own field log resolves the discrepancy. Core JOHNL1 penetrated 3.4 m in 4 m of water. Beierle logged a 108 cm package between the 124 and 232 cm marks, measured upward from the core base. It contains abundant charcoal. Peat containing slope-washed sediment and dispersed ash overlies it from 232 to 280 cm. The investigator explicitly attributes the excess ash to secondary deposition and mixing with material carried into the basin. The core was destroyed before laboratory analyses could be performed. Its lithologic log and photograph survive. [Beierle (1997), pp. 58–61, Figs. 5.8–5.9](https://hdl.handle.net/1880/26787) · [Buckland compilation](../buckland2020-293-localities.csv)

Nearby profiles establish why a regional 10 cm estimate cannot replace individual measurements. Upper Kananaskis core UKANL1 has a discrete **2 cm** bed at marks 94–96. Lake Louise core LL1 has **7 cm** between 521 and 528. Beierle’s Copper Lake core has **1 cm** at 259–260. These are surviving beds in distinct basins. At Frederick Lake, on the other hand, the **28 cm** package at 270–298 contains seeds and abundant charcoal. The investigator identifies both initial fallout and later slopewash. Sibbald Lake has an ash-rich **50 cm** unit. At Cartwright Lake the 10 cm interval contains dispersed ash in peaty sediment directly above an erosional unconformity. Beierle identifies tephras by optical examination and their stratigraphic positions; subsequent laboratory work was lost for some cores. No single-grain chemical fingerprint was reported for these reads. [Beierle, methods pp. 31–32 and individual core logs](https://ucalgary.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/7b299cfb-2da5-4b59-8344-631fb3173616/content)
