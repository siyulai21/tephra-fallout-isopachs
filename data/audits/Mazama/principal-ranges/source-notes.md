# Source readings and field controls

Buckland et al. classify deposition in Supplement 1. The workbook’s site number, Excel row, reported value and qualifier identify the original entry. Their P classification supplies evidence for primary deposition even where an original investigator’s report remains unavailable. The class applies to deposition at that locality. Individual portions of a section may still contain reworked sediment, multiple eruptions or pyroclastic flow. The archived field ledger documents these cases. [Paper and supplements](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00445-020-1362-1) · [Original workbook](../../../sources/mazama/buckland2020-esm1.xlsx) · [Preceding source and facies audit](../distal-review/README.md)

## Cypress Hills, Alberta

Robertson’s Table 4.5 on p. 165 gives two contacts, test identity and depositional interpretation for 13 ash occurrences. Eleven are classified primary. Appendix 8, Table A8.1 gives NAD83 UTM zone 12N coordinates. The [test transcription](cypress-sections.csv) preserves each original position, stratigraphic interval and context. The principal input groups closely spaced tests by the named study locality. In a group, the smallest and largest primary bed thicknesses define one site interval. The model position is an actual test location. The H soundings span roughly 0.6 km. The two Q cores are one metre apart. This grouping limits the statistical influence of an intensively augered site. [Robertson (2006), full dissertation](https://hdl.handle.net/1880/101762)

At H and Q, Robertson reports 4–12 and 7–9 cm, respectively. These intervals include the 9 cm layers in CH2001-52 and -58. Freeman’s depth markers of 441 and 305 cm lie within those layers. The T and W primary augers give 12 and 8 cm. At X (Stampede), Robertson identifies 10, 12 and 30 cm primary beds, despite erosion elsewhere on the fan. These new field observations justify reopening the previously omitted Stampede locality. The 15 cm Jensen sample remains a related observation there. Robertson’s possibly secondary bands at E and T are preserved in the transcription. [Robertson, Table 4.5 and section 4.4.2](https://hdl.handle.net/1880/101762) · [Freeman et al., locations H and Q](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256090593_Radiocarbon_Age_Estimates_from_the_SCAPE_Project_2000-2005)

## Dog Lake, Cobb Lake and Kootenay Pond, British Columbia

Hallett’s Dog Lake core has Mazama at 177–200 cm and contains the dated charcoal TO-5192. The core log supplies **23 cm**. Jensen’s cited 23 cm is consistent with the investigator’s core. Buckland’s 10 cm remains in the source column. Hallett’s Appendix 2 documents the chemistry for both Dog and Cobb. Kootenay Pond has a likely correlation from its distinct grey bed and stratigraphic comparison. ## Additional checks

- Kuehn et al., Table S1 site 81, list Creston and Wilbur as the same fen, associate both sets of field investigators and document conflicting published positions. S1 site 120 contributes the 24 cm auger bed. Site 121 and its 5 cm reading stay linked to it. Kuehn site 61 also resolves the correct study lake for the 10 cm Tepee Lake entry. Kuehn’s Glacier Peak bed measurements concern a separate eruption. [Kuehn et al., original site supplement](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn%3Acambridge.org%3Aid%3Aarticle%3AS0033589400005779/resource/name/S0033589400005779sup001.pdf)
- At Lower Decker Lake, Whitlock et al. recovered a 3.00 m long core and identified Mazama by electron microprobe. S1 puts the ash at 247 cm. At Lake Washington, Leopold et al. describe an 11 m core. The compiled gyttja dates above and below the ash are inverted. The **300 and 1,100 cm** outer limits assume the whole local fall lies within the recovered sequence. They are particularly weak constraints. Neither coring depth nor sampling resolution provides a bed measurement. [Whitlock et al., coring and chronology](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0033589410001092) · [Leopold et al.](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.218.4579.1305) · [Sample-age crosswalk](../egan-age-crosswalk.csv)
- In a separate Washington core, Tsukada and Sugita give both contacts of Mazama O at **9.24 and 9.16 m** in Mineral Lake. The bed is **8 cm** thick. Their age model and the dates below and above support the attribution. Their four Mount St. Helens horizons have distinct depth intervals. The named lake represents the core position. [Tsukada and Sugita (1982), pp. 402–403](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02489477)
- The Seattle I-90 investigators describe a **2–3 inch** inferred Mazama bed in two peat samples along 4th Avenue South, equivalent to **5.08–7.62 cm**. Fig. 1 locates the road alignment near South Royal Brougham Way. The interval assumes the ash in the compressed peat retained its local thickness. At both Seattle and Mineral Lake the coordinates identify the project or lake, with the exact boring position unresolved. [Washington DGER Bulletin 78, pp. 845–848](https://dnr.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-04/ger_b78_engineering_geol_v2_pt2of4.pdf)
- For pre-existing ranges, the row crosswalk retains the original observer, archive filename, table or figure locator, and competing interpretations. The Mount Baker RS–15 section, for example, explicitly assigns **5–20 cm** to layer O within soil below a separate lahar. At Upper Kirk Road the **35–40 cm** upper pumice unit is one airborne component. At Nordegg the **2–5 cm** total contains a reworked top. The latter two sites yield one-sided constraints on original whole-eruption fallout. [Mount Baker USGS Professional Paper 1865, Appendix 1](https://doi.org/10.3133/pp1865) · [Range decisions](range-review.csv)
