## Cores where the ash was invisible

The SO95 expedition inspected the box-core surface for volcanic ash and opened the front of each box to describe its vertical section. Section 3.4, Table 1 records ash, overlying water, surface disturbance and surficial organic material. Direct inspection of the original table matters. For example, 17956 has a 1 mm bed on a washed surface, 17957 has a dash, and 17958 has fine patches. At 17954 the surface table gives a dash on a washed-out core, while the dedicated tephra study records patchy ash below 1 mm. The tephra study takes precedence there. Above a basal undisturbed layer, a surficial ash-free appearance can coexist with a mixed deposit. The detection of a 1 mm layer in one box establishes capability under those conditions. The 0.0015 cm discussed in the early study concerns dilution by background sedimentation. Neither reading sets an upper limit across the survey. [SO95 shipboard table, PDF p. 74](https://oceanrep.geomar.de/33282/1/Report%20SO95.pdf#page=74) · [Literal surface observations](so95-surface.csv) · [Marine decisions and site positions](marine-site-decisions.csv)

At GIK17949, 17951, 17952 and 17962 there was clear overlying water, a smooth surface with retained fluff, and an identifiable lower boundary of the bioturbated sediment over homogeneous clay. These records support a weak inventory ceiling if the local fallout remained above that boundary, bulk packing is comparable and lateral export was negligible. Allow the entire overlying package to be ash. Multiply the maximum recovered core length from the spade-box register by the overlying fraction of the drawn column. For GIK17949 the picked top, deepest mixed contact and core bottom are at y = 896, 950 and 995. Allowing two pixels outward at each edge gives 45 × (952 − 894)/(993 − 894) = 26.36 cm, rounded up to **27 cm**. The other three give **43, 38 and 20 cm**. These limits constrain only very thick predicted deposits. The source describes winnowing along the northern slope, and several other casts have washed, cracked or soupy surfaces, burrowing extending to the base, or insufficient evidence of a retained pre-event substrate. Those readings supply survey checks. A loss of local ash destroys the transfer even in an intact recovered box. The omission diagnostic tests the four ceilings together. ## Luzon

David et al. sampled seven identifiable, stratified sections of the coarse pumice from June 15. Each supplies a minimum for combined climactic fallout if that component is primary at the section. The earlier audit already located the sections on the authors' town-scale schematic. Independent settlement checks differ by up to 2.52 km. Nearby markers from other surveys can see the same local fallout variation. No location was redigitized for these sections. [David et al., Table 1 and Fig. 3](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pinatubo/david/index.html) · [Seven positions](../survey-coverage/david-schematic.csv)

Quimbo's 2017–18 soil pits preserve the buried pre-1991 soil, a pumice bed and fine ash or younger soil. Four groups preserve a primary pumice component, giving local minimum thicknesses of 6 cm in the forest, 3 cm in the rice field, 8 cm in the low grassland and 9 cm in the high grassland. ## Source-image reproduction

The original reports and their SHA-256 digests are recorded in the [source manifest](source-manifest.csv). Extract Quimbo PDF page 282 with `pdfimages -f 282 -l 282 -j quimbo2020.pdf quimbo-map`. Composite the extracted JPEG over white using the companion monochrome soft mask as opacity, and save the resulting 788 × 493 RGB image as `farm-source.png`. Render the untrimmed SO95 PDF page 76 at 105 dpi with `pdftoppm -f 76 -l 76 -r 105 -png so95.pdf prof`. The source plates and all crop centres use these image coordinates. Run `python -m benchmarks.pinatubo_review_figures <image-directory>` to redraw the annotated plates.
