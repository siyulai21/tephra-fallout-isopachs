# Pinatubo climactic Layer C · 24 November 2026

The principal dataset contains **235 localities** for the June 15, 1991 climactic fallout. The CSV stores accumulated bulk thickness in centimetres and coordinates as longitude and latitude. It has 147 point values, 18 two-sided intervals, 31 lower bounds and 39 upper bounds. The point values include a trap-derived bulk equivalent and three reconstructions of original ash thickness. The earlier 208-row input remains available for the five-method comparison. The curator's visual confirmation of Koyaguchi Figure 3 and Paladio-Melosantos Figure 7 was carried forward. The accepted coordinates, values, and bound directions from those figures are preserved. The revision adds 27 localities and revises the representation of 18 existing ones. Every interval has both endpoints in the likelihood. Each held-out interval is scored by its predicted probability mass between the two endpoints. ## Cores and the isopach edge

An inspected core without a visible bed constrains visibility at the time of recovery. Inferring original local fallout also requires information about retention, bioturbation, recovery and the observation threshold. At SO132-47, for example, the survey recorded no visible bed while the X-ray section identified completely mixed ash. Its quantitative entry uses the investigator's 1.5 cm original-thickness estimate. Replacing it with a 2 cm ceiling tests a weaker preservation analogy. Four SO95 cores support much weaker ceilings from their surface sediment packages. All 151 archive entries carry their own position and observation type, including possible duplicate map marks and named casts. Only the accepted CSV supplies rows to the model. ## Search coverage and outstanding records

The search followed the citations and station names in the existing Koyaguchi, Paladio-Melosantos, David, Wiesner and Wang, Wiesner et al., Hess, Wetzel and Marquez inventories. It also covered SONNE cruise reports and core registers, trap studies, geochemistry, Luzon farm and soil stratigraphy, eruption chronology and descriptions of airfields and distant fallout. Retained candidates, excluded observations and the corresponding source material are linked below. [Earlier evidence inventory](../full-evidence.csv) · [Existing source files](../../../sources/pinatubo-1991/README.md) · [New source fingerprints](source-manifest.csv)

- **Offshore.** The SO95 shipboard observations supply the condition of 45 station entries representing 46 positioned casts. The SÜDMEER lithologies help partition the nearshore deposits. The Hess audit retains all 30 station groups, including questioned values and a displaced revisit. Haeckel et al. studied six SO132 stations including 27, 40 and 41. Their examples of beds exceeding 6 cm at stations 40 and 41 cross-check the station-40 group and the direct 6.1 cm at station 41. The complete geochemistry table was unavailable. The later SCS-C trap archive represents the same mooring. A faunal observation or a core deployment alone supplies no original bed thickness. Wang's 1999 dissertation and the complete SO132 original station ledger remain outstanding. Wetzel’s 2008 Figure 5B supplies the original 5 cm at SO114-4. The September 2008 SEPM data index and the Basel repository entry did not yield the full supplementary station list. [Wetzel, original paper](https://doi.org/10.2110/palo.2007.p07-096r) · [Basel record](https://hdl.handle.net/20.500.14716/63596) · [SEPM archive](https://www.sepm.org/palaios-supplementary-data-1) [Haeckel et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-821X%2801%2900496-4) · [Later SCS-C series](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/ntmsbrs3fm/1) · [Marine field review](source-notes.md)
- **Luzon.** Quimbo's original contacts and positions support four grouped pumice minima. The farm table distinguishes recollections, excavated material and thicknesses copied from an isopach. Remobilized ash and lahar sites document transport. The seven accepted ash-farm ceilings retain their transfer assumptions. [Soil pit worksheet](quimbo-pits.csv) · [Farm worksheet](quimbo-farms.csv) · [Field evidence and reading cards](source-notes.md#luzon)
- **Independent descriptions.** Wolfe and Hoblitt's Fig. 14 describes 0.8 cm of early ash and 4.2 cm of climactic pumice near Santa Catalina about 36 km southeast of the vent. The existing P7-151 marker has the same 4.2 cm at 35.2 km in that sector. The description corroborates the measurement but does not locate a distinct second exposure. Fig. 13 records roughly 9 cm of climactic fallout in the eastern Clark area, where several exposures already enter the survey. Exact photo-section coordinates were unavailable. [Wolfe and Hoblitt, Figs. 13–14](https://pubs.usgs.gov/pinatubo/wolfe/index.html)
- **Distal and proximal limits.** Singapore particle identifications, Sabah and Sarawak reports, eyewitness descriptions, roof loading and town-wide cumulative deposits provide occurrence or contextual checks when the site, sampled area, phase thickness or local bulk conversion is unresolved. The reported 1–2 m average within 3 km of the rim remains a regional observation.
