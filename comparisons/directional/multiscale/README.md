# Directional isopachs from the vent to the distal deposit

The multiscale directional ensemble is the principal directional reconstruction for all nine accepted eruptions. The same policy fits 1,091 quantitative locations, including lower limits, upper limits and ranges. It draws a coherent field with a direction that can change with distance, estimates local differences across neighboring sites and integrates the median-thickness surface over Earth. ## What changed in the fit

In the previous model, slopes changed across interpolation triangles. Outside the triangulated area each sampled bearing could influence the range of a thin deposit. Those mechanisms aligned corners, narrow protrusions and indentations across adjacent contours. Interpolating a few thick proximal measurements also produced polygonal near-vent contours. The new model fits low-order angular variation with a finite proximal core, changing fan headings and a spatial covariance that grows with radius in both local directions. A broad, training-only range prior limits continuation into empty sectors. Documentation and published packets give the common length, spread, local-resolution and likelihood assumptions. [Method](../../../docs/methods/directional-multiscale.md)

Several alternatives exposed the tradeoff. The first continuous field used a radial coordinate transformation that gave an isolated YTT site a long narrow zone of influence. It also extended Aso-4 southward. A broad field that could drop local structure underfit Minoan's inner deposit. Allowing station-scale effects everywhere produced small loops around individual cores. Retaining only neighborhood averages underfit their contrasts. The adopted field shares half of the resolvable small-scale contrast with the deposit and puts the remaining site-scale variance in the observation model. All five development stages have matched partition records. These eruptions informed method development. The cross-validation comparisons are retrospective, and the frozen surveys and simulations provide additional tests of transfer. [Partition scores and numerical checks for the five successive development stages](development-ablations.csv.gz)

Across nine full-data sensitivity fits at each endpoint, mean absolute log-thickness error was 0.387 with only neighborhood-average structure and 0.264 with all available finer-scale contrast shared spatially. The principal half-sharing policy gave 0.323. Against that principal fit, the two endpoint alternatives changed the central map volume by less than 5% in each eruption. Site fidelity can therefore change considerably with little change in bulk volume. The model cannot determine how much of a single exposure is representative of its surroundings. ## Geometry across thickness levels

We extracted filled exceedance polygons at doubling levels and repeated the extraction on two independent Cartesian resolutions (193 and 385 points on the long side). All components and holes contribute. Perimeter-weighted total absolute turning measures cumulative bending, with one turn for a convex closed line. At the finer resolution, across the **78 eruption–level pairs present in both editions**, its median fell from **2.68 to 1.27 turns** and the eruption-specific median fell in each of the nine cases. Median worst radial indentation decreased from **0.278 to 0.121** of the convex-hull radius where a vent-connected comparison was possible. High-order variation in log radius decreased from 0.071 to 0.033. The median absolute change in successive log contour spacing decreased from 0.059 to 0.044. These diagnostics describe the rendered shapes. A real deposit can have a sharp flank, a detached patch or a changing direction. [Both resolutions, every contour and status](geometry.csv.gz)

Median eccentricity on those levels changed from 0.727 to 0.746. The dominant axis, adjacent-level rotation, component count, holes, vent containment and spacing on common bearings are included in the geometry table. Axis changes are scored only when both eccentricities are at least 0.4, since angles of nearly circular contours are unstable. The revised maps retain up to four connected components at a level. ## Known deposits and independent surveys

Two seeds and two independent spatial scrambles were used for the simulated deposits. The rotated circular-deposit test used one common noise realization with sampling spokes rotated 23°. The estimated 1 cm radius varied by 7.3% of its mean around the circle in both orientations. The revised method recovered 108% of a smooth fan's reference volume, 98% for two matched fans and 92% for a broad umbrella. It recovered 77% for turning fans and 68% when a sector was unsampled. In the indistinguishable hidden-fan experiment, the exact same observations give **111% of the visible-only truth and 51% of the truth with an additional lobe**. No geometric rule can resolve those alternatives from these inputs.

The conditional 95% whole-volume intervals contained all 18 specified truths across 16 fits. The two hidden-lobe datasets illustrate how wide those intervals can be. The same pair of competing totals (33.6 and 72.8 km³) was compatible with intervals of 30–1,045 and 30–504 km³. One multilobe run reached the integration ceiling without meeting the replication target. This small designed experiment establishes neither 95% coverage in nature nor a tight upper bound. Geographic smoothness also had a cost at low thickness. Against the previous matched directional model, mean false-positive 1 cm area increased from 24,549 to 48,572 km² for the smooth deposit and from 16,411 to 43,797 km² for the missing-sector deposit. Both false and missed area, volume ratios and overlap at 0.5, 1, 10 and 100 cm are in the tables. Consequently a long smooth outer contour still needs independent field evidence.
